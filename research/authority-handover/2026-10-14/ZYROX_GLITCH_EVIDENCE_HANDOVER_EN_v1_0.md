## 1. Executive summary

**GLITCH — CONFIRMED:** On 12 October 2026 at **17:51:18 UTC**, six GLITCH token accounts transferred a combined **116,513,762.335674 GLITCH** into a single receiving token account in **one Solana transaction** (slot **456135125**, outer instructions **2–7**). The transaction had **seven signers**, comprising six source authorities and the receiving account owner/fee payer. Five additional sources contributed **46,513,762.335674 GLITCH** alongside a previously identified 70-million-token transfer. The six source balances were emptied; the receiving token balance increased from **2,936,950.790841** to **119,450,713.126515 GLITCH**. This demonstrates coordinated transaction execution, **not** common human control. [G1]

**ZYROX × GLITCH — CONFIRMED wallet-address overlap:** Exactly the same public key, `6hR7GwHpdHbJgNJAagm64KMsMYu8mR6ExzmbE5N8wv1N`, occurs in the ZYROX evidence and received **435.460959353 SOL** from a GLITCH-linked counterparty on 12 October at **17:52:55 UTC**. The sender's funds were commingled. The full transfer cannot be assigned exclusively to GLITCH sale proceeds. Common ownership and final beneficiary are **UNRESOLVED**. [G2]

**Exchange-infrastructure lead — CONFIRMED transfers, SUPPORTED attribution:** Four historical native SOL transfers from the case-linked U300 key totaled **472.929423997 SOL**. A separate 56HEQ-authorized transfer sent **995.831502 USDT** to a token account historically owned by the same recipient key, `5tzFkiKscXHK5ZXCGbXZxdw7gTjjD1mBwuoFbhUvuAi9`. Solscan and Bitquery publicly associate that recipient with Binance infrastructure; Binance itself has not provided a historical custody or internal customer-booking confirmation. September transfers predate the investigated launches, and the October transfer is not uniquely attributable to GLITCH proceeds. [B1]

**Investigative utility:** This package provides exact transaction identifiers, time, amounts, address-level relationships, archived receipts, and questions that an authorized investigator could refer to relevant custodians. It is **not** evidence identifying an exchange customer or a named individual responsible for either launch.

## 2. Evidence classifications

| Level | Meaning |

An on-chain signature establishes authorization by a cryptographic key, not a person's identity. A payment to an exchange-labeled address does not by itself establish a customer deposit, a credited trading account, a KYC subject, or the ultimate beneficiary.

## 6. Questions for a legally authorized exchange inquiry

For BE001–BE005, an investigating authority could request, subject to applicable law:

1. Whether Binance controlled the recipient public key and relevant USDT token account at each historical transaction time, and for what operational purpose.
2. Whether each transfer was internally booked as a customer deposit, treasury transfer, internal sweep, uncredited payment, or another category, with relevant internal reference identifiers.
3. If any customer account was credited, which legally disclosable account records, instruction histories and relevant KYC records correspond to the transfer.
4. Whether U300, 56HEQ, and the separately identified fee payer were themselves part of exchange-controlled infrastructure.
5. Preservation of relevant transaction, custody, booking, and operational logs through a formally authorized request.

**No request has been made to Binance by NPCsignals.** This report does not assume a specific response or that every public transfer maps to an exchange customer.

## 7. Promotional claims and potential complainant material

The archived ZYROX claim evidence includes promotional language regarding a purported absence of rug pulls or pump-and-dump activity, majority supply locking, and a stated market capitalization target. These claims are relevant to an independently conducted comparison between statements and observable on-chain behavior. The existence of a promotional claim and substantial selling does **not alone** establish deliberate misrepresentation or a criminal offense. [M1]

For GLITCH, the exact original source and timestamp of purported explicit promises such as “fair launch” or “no insiders” remain **UNRESOLVED**. Such wording must not be attributed as a verified primary-source promise without an original post or an authenticated capture.

A person wishing to make a formal complaint should separately supply the competent authority with:
- Their own purchase and sale signatures, transaction dates, assets, quantities and amounts.
- The exact promotional statements they relied upon, with original URLs, message IDs, preserved screenshots and dates where available.
- A concise explanation of the statements' role in their decision to buy.
- Their own financial records sufficient to substantiate actual expenditure and any loss.
- Personal identification and contact details **only through private official channels**, not through this public repository.

NPCsignals does not collect or publish complainants' personal details as part of this report.

## 9. Conclusion

The public record establishes a coordinated GLITCH token consolidation, an exact wallet-address overlap between the ZYROX and GLITCH investigation networks, and separate historical transfers into infrastructure labeled by independent providers as Binance-associated.

It does **not** establish common human control, a particular beneficial owner, that historical exchange transfers contain proceeds from the later token launches, or whether any participant committed fraud.

The technical objective is to provide a verifiable investigative starting point and document the boundary at which legally authorized service-provider records may become necessary.
