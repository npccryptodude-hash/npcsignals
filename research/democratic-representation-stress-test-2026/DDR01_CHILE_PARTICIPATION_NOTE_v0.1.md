## Why Chile matters

Chile is unusually informative for DDR-01 because qualifying foreign nationals can vote without first becoming Chilean citizens. This makes the country analytically different from Norway, Sweden, Germany, Great Britain and the United States.

The case is strongest for:
- electorate composition;
- turnout;
- origin heterogeneity;
- political incorporation.

It is not yet strong enough for national party-choice alignment.

## Electoral eligibility

Servel reported a provisional 2025 electorate of:
- 15,791,056 total eligible voters;
- 886,190 eligible foreign nationals.

That is approximately 5.6% of the provisional electorate.

Largest foreign-national electorates:
- Venezuela: 237,889
- Peru: 193,883
- Colombia: 103,239

This is an official administrative count, not a survey estimate.

## Participation evidence

Recent peer-reviewed research using Chilean administrative electoral data shows persistent participation gaps between Chilean-born and foreign-born residents.

Herrera & Morales (2026) use an administrative census covering 2017–2020 with more than 30 million observations, including 645,000 foreign-born observations.

They report:
- Chilean nationals were about eight times more likely to be party members;
- Chilean nationals voted about 2.5 times as often as foreign-born residents;
- among foreign-born party members, membership leaned more left;
- turnout among foreign-born party members was highest among those affiliated with right-wing parties.

Important:
party membership is not the same as ballot choice.

## DDR mapping

DDR1 — Eligible-electorate share  
**STRONG / OFFICIAL**  
Foreign nationals were roughly 5.6% of the provisional 2025 electorate.

DDR2 — Turnout gap  
**STRONG / ADMINISTRATIVE**  
Foreign-born residents participate at substantially lower rates than nationals in the available administrative evidence.

DDR3 — Party/bloc alignment  
**UNRESOLVED**  
Party-membership evidence cannot be substituted for actual ballot choice.

DDR4 — Origin heterogeneity  
**SUPPORTED**  
The electorate is highly heterogeneous by country of origin and integration profile.

DDR5 — Alignment trajectory  
**OPEN FOR PARTY CHOICE / MEASURABLE FOR PARTICIPATION**

DDR6 — Counter-mobilization  
**OPEN**  
No current causal estimate should be inferred merely from the presence of a large foreign electorate.

DDR7 — Outcome relevance  
**NOT ASSIGNABLE YET**  
Without defensible party-choice data, the size of the foreign electorate cannot be translated into net bloc contribution.

## Main finding

Chile demonstrates an important DDR-01 boundary:

> A demographic group can be electorally large and administratively measurable while its partisan effect remains unknown.

That is not a data failure. It is a distinction between participation evidence and vote-choice evidence.

## Methodological value

Chile strengthens three parts of DDR-01:

1. eligibility rules matter;
2. turnout can sharply reduce the political weight implied by electorate share;
3. party membership or ideological affiliation cannot be treated as equivalent to the secret ballot.
