NPCsignals — Democratic Representation Stress Test 2026
Methods v1.0

Purpose

The project tests how political preferences are translated into governing influence and how that process interacts with institutional restraint, political voice, trust, information integrity, and electoral realignment.

It is not designed to identify the “best” or “worst” democracy and does not produce a single composite democracy score.

Sample

15 fixed countries:
Norway, Sweden, Denmark, Germany, United Kingdom, France, Italy, Poland, United States, Costa Rica, Chile, Ghana, Botswana, Japan, Thailand.

Time structure

Historical baseline: 2000–2015
Pre-shock reference: 2016–2023
Main contemporary observation: 2024–2026
Longitudinal continuation: through 2028

Contemporary Mandate Integrity eligibility

A scored contemporary case must have its qualifying decision or event inside the observation window.

The primary eligibility cutoff was frozen at September 10, 2026. The Swedish general election of September 13, 2026 was pre-registered as the final case before its outcome was known and is therefore retained as a transparent exception.

Historical events can be retained as controls but do not contribute to the contemporary country score.

Case-selection rule

Each country receives six cases:

2 Electoral / Power Translation
2 Major Policy / Fiscal / Social
2 Institutional / High Persistence

This stratification was introduced after the three-case pilot showed material sensitivity to case composition.

Case qualification

A case should normally satisfy at least two of the following:

- significant economic reach
- institutional or constitutional significance
- effects beyond one electoral term
- high reversibility cost
- clear importance for power distribution or public mandate

Mandate Integrity variables

Each case receives a 0–4 score on:

1. Mandate strength — 20%
2. Public alignment — 15%
3. Pre-legislative inclusion — 10%
4. Legislative depth — 15%
5. Institutional restraint — 20%
6. Reversibility safeguard — 10%
7. Mandate-persistence legitimacy — 10%

Interpretive anchors

4 — strong / clearly satisfied
3 — broadly satisfied / some reservations
2 — materially mixed / unresolved
1 — serious deficiency
0 — direct structural failure

Temporal Mandate Freshness

A subcriterion of Mandate Strength:

4 — fresh/current explicit mandate
3 — current general mandate
2 — old/ambiguous or post-election caretaker period
1 — clearly superseded public mandate
0 — no meaningful electoral mandate

Output bands

85–100 HEALTHY
70–84 WATCH
50–69 STRESS
0–49 FAILURE

These bands describe case-based Mandate Integrity stress only.

Non-compensatory safeguard test

The first-pass score is compensatory: strong cases can offset weak cases numerically.

A separate safeguard test will examine whether repeated structural failure in Electoral / Power Translation should place a ceiling on the overall classification. This rule has not yet been adopted and will be tested symmetrically across all countries after the 90-case ledger is complete.

Dimension II — Political Voice / Representation

PV1 — perceived political voice
PV2 — consultation responsiveness
PV3 — electoral-to-power translation
PV4 — voice trajectory

Where identical survey items are unavailable, proxy measures are explicitly labelled and are not treated as directly comparable percentages.

Dimension IV — Information Integrity

Information stress is split by mechanism:

I-A — covert partisan communication
I-B — coordinated foreign influence
I-C — persistent corrected falsehoods in an open information system
I-D — legal restriction of political expression
I-E — political pressure on public-service media

Ordinary political spin, disagreement, or a single inaccurate statement is not sufficient for a high-stress classification.

Dimension V — Trust & Legitimacy

T1 — government trust
T2 — parliamentary trust
T3 — party trust
T4 — court / rule-of-law trust

Support for democracy as a governing principle is treated separately from satisfaction with current leaders or institutions.

Dimension VI — Polarization / Realignment

PR1 — electoral realignment
PR2 — challenger growth
PR3 — governing translation
PR4 — delegitimizing polarization

Political movement to the left or right cannot itself lower a democratic classification.

Each candidate finding is stored with:

- claim

- evidence strength
- falsification condition

Historical falsification rule

A contemporary mechanism is tested against the 2000–2015 baseline and 2016–2023 reference period.

The aim is to determine whether an apparent contemporary pattern is genuinely new, an intensification of an older mechanism, or simply a stable property of the political system.

CDF-07 revision

An earlier version implied that a new preference-to-power problem may have emerged recently. Historical comparison partly falsified that interpretation.

The revised candidate is:

“The institutional mechanisms that translate voter preference into governing power are often long-standing. Their democratic consequences become more visible when electoral realignment grows large enough to stress those mechanisms.”

Audit discipline

- original judgments are not silently overwritten
- historical controls remain separate from contemporary scoring
- uncertainty is stated rather than forced into a category
- political actors are assessed under the same procedural rules regardless of ideology
- court review is not automatically mandate override
- coalition exclusion is not automatically mandate override
- dissatisfaction is not automatically democratic rejection
- political realignment is not automatically democratic decline
- press concerns are not automatically information manipulation

Versioning

Material methodological changes must be documented before any new final scoring pass.
