## what is this study asking?

winning an election, representing voters and having the power to govern are different things. this study asks how those differences work in 15 selected countries. it also examines how courts, parliaments and other institutions limit governing power.

this is an exploratory comparison, not a global ranking of democracies.

## what has actually been documented?

- a common method and a register of 90 selected cases: six per country, covering elections, major policies and institutional decisions;
- dated corrections to case selection, event dates and the stage reached by proposed laws;
- a completed **case-design** checkpoint. this does not mean the cases have received final numerical scores.

the [latest case register, version 1.2](AUDIT_LEDGER_v1.2.md) leaves all seven scoring fields blank. its source-pack labels name the intended evidence providers; they are not a completed claim-by-claim source register.

## what remains uncertain?

a resolved design is not a verified result for every case. the 90-case register still requires its final source register and numerical scoring before readers can reproduce a comparative score. existing qualitative findings do not establish a universal causal explanation of political distrust or governing performance.
