## What is frozen

The DDR-01 research question, seven variables, evidence tiers, direct/indirect mechanism split, causal-language guardrails and comparison rules are frozen at v0.1.

New data may change findings. New countries may change the comparative picture. The rules should not be changed in response to whether later evidence strengthens or weakens the working hypothesis.

## Research question

Do immigrant-origin and minority electorates show persistent party/bloc alignment, does that alignment weaken or strengthen over time, and can demographic change also produce counter-mobilization among other voters?

## Frozen mechanism split

DIRECT EFFECT:
Eligible immigrant-origin/minority electorate × turnout × party choice.

INDIRECT EFFECT:
Change in turnout or party choice among the wider electorate attributable to immigration/demographic exposure.

The two effects may point in opposite directions.

## Frozen evidence tiers

A — Direct individual-level / validated / official election-study evidence.  
B — Strong representative subgroup evidence.  
C — Aggregate/ecological evidence or credible indirect-mechanism evidence.  
D — Insufficient / non-comparable.

Country-specific categories are retained rather than artificially harmonized when definitions differ.

## Frozen guardrails

1. Do not treat immigrant, foreign-born, naturalized, ethnic-minority, religious-minority and second-generation categories as interchangeable.
2. Document voting eligibility separately from population share.
3. Separate turnout from vote preference.
4. Aggregate neighbourhood results cannot prove individual vote choice.
5. Disaggregate origin groups where evidence shows meaningful divergence.
6. Do not infer missing ethnicity/origin data from names, religion or neighbourhood without a validated research design.
7. Do not translate national vote differences directly into seats in district-based systems.
8. Do not claim that a demographic group "decided" an election without a defensible counterfactual.
9. Historical causal evidence may establish a mechanism but not automatically a current national effect.
10. DDR-01 is a realignment module, not a democratic-quality index and not part of MIS scoring.

## Frozen causal-language rule

Allowed:
- "material contribution"
- "associated with"
- "historically supported mechanism"
- "outcome relevance"
- "counter-mobilization evidence"
- "net direction not identified"

Requires stronger identification:
- "caused the result"
- "decided the election"
- "immigration moved the country right/left"
- "citizenship policy produced the victory"

## Working model at freeze

The strongest current formulation is:

> Demographic change can alter electoral competition through at least two distinct channels: the direct voting behaviour of immigrant-origin/minority electorates and indirect responses elsewhere in the electorate. The strength and direction of both channels vary by origin composition, turnout, geography, party system and time.

This is a working comparative model, not an NPCsignal.

## Falsification discipline

The module should actively search for cases that weaken this model, including:
- immigrant-origin electorates with no persistent party difference;
- countries with demographic change but no identifiable counter-mobilization;
- cases where class, education, age or geography fully explain the apparent demographic relationship;
- repeated elections where the observed alignment disappears;
- high-quality evidence contradicting current subgroup patterns.

Null results remain in the archive.

## Versioning

Future versions:
- v0.2 may add evidence or correct sources without changing frozen concepts.
- v1.0 requires a completed cross-country evidence audit and explicit falsification pass.

Any future methodological change must be logged as a version change and justified independently of the resulting political direction.
