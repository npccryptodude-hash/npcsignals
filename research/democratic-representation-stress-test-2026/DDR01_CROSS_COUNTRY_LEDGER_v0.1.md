## Purpose

This ledger standardizes the DDR-01 evidence already collected across the 15-country Democratic Representation Stress Test sample.

The module measures two distinct channels:

1. DIRECT EFFECT — how immigrant-origin / minority / naturalized electorates themselves vote.
2. INDIRECT EFFECT — whether immigration or demographic exposure changes turnout or party choice in the wider electorate.

No country receives a generic "immigration effect" score.

Evidence tiers:
A — direct individual-level / validated / official election-study evidence  
B — strong representative subgroup survey evidence  
C — aggregate/ecological or strong indirect evidence  
D — insufficient / non-comparable for the focal measure

## Falsification / stress conditions for the next pass

The working model must be weakened if additional comparable cases show that:

1. broad immigrant-origin status adds little explanatory value after origin, class, age, religion and geography are controlled;
2. direct alignment differences disappear over repeated elections;
3. turnout composition does not materially alter observed group-party relationships;
4. credible causal studies fail to replicate counter-mobilization outside the historical Denmark/Italy settings;
5. apparent demographic effects are better explained by geography, education, income, age or candidate effects;
6. outcome-relevance estimates prove highly unstable under reasonable assumptions;
7. countries with similar demographic change show no recurring mechanism once measurement differences are controlled.
