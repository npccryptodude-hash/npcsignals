## Research question

When does demographic background retain an electoral relationship after turnout, origin composition, geography, social composition and political mediation are taken into account?

## Core model

DDR-01 separates two channels:

1. **Direct channel** — participation and party choice among immigrant-origin, minority or naturalized electorates.
2. **Indirect channel** — changes in turnout or party choice elsewhere in the electorate associated with immigration or demographic exposure.

Neither channel has a fixed ideological direction.

## Main findings

1. A homogeneous "immigrant vote" model is rejected.
2. Left-of-centre alignment exists in several systems, but it is neither universal nor stable.
3. Origin composition can conceal opposite partisan patterns.
4. Turnout composition can generate aggregate realignment without large-scale individual switching.
5. Aggregate national alignment can coexist with strong local or subgroup defection.
6. Historical counter-mobilization is causally documented in Denmark and Italy, but contemporary magnitude is conditional and unresolved.
7. Broad background status sometimes retains predictive value after controls, but composition and political mediation remain substantial.
8. Outcome relevance does not establish that a demographic group uniquely caused an election result.

## Falsified alternatives

Rejected as universal rules:
- "immigrants vote left";
- "immigration moves the wider electorate right";
- "demographic realignment mainly means party switching";
- "a contribution larger than a winning margin proves a group decided the election";
- "immigrant-origin status alone explains the pattern."

## Robustness

The direct-channel conclusions survive:
- removal of Sweden;
- removal of Great Britain;
- removal of Germany;
- removal of ecological evidence;
- A/B-only restriction;
- 2020–2026-only restriction;
- removal of NPCsignals model-derived vote estimates.

The indirect channel weakens materially when historical Denmark/Italy causal studies are removed.

## Nulls preserved

No forced direction is assigned where evidence is insufficient:
- France DDR3 open;
- Poland DDR3 insufficient;
- Costa Rica DDR3 insufficient;
- Chile DDR3 unresolved;
- Japan DDR3 insufficient;
- Denmark/Italy contemporary DDR7 unresolved.

## v1.0 conclusion

> Demographic background can remain electorally relevant after some compositional controls, but neither direct demographic voting nor indirect electorate response has a fixed ideological direction. Turnout, origin composition, geography, political attitudes, party supply and electoral institutions shape how demographic change translates into political outcomes.
