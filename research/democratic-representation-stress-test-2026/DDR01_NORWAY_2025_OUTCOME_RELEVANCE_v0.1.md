# DDR-01 Norway 2025 — Outcome Relevance Pass v0.1

Status: PROVISIONAL DESCRIPTIVE ESTIMATE  
Parent module: DDR-01 // Demographic Voting Alignment  
Purpose: Estimate the electoral relevance of voters with immigrant background in Norway's 2025 Storting election without making unsupported causal claims.

## 1. Electorate size and turnout

Statistics Norway (SSB) reports that in 2025:

- immigrants made up 9.6% of eligible voters;
- Norwegian-born voters with two immigrant parents made up 1.9%;
- combined, these groups were about 11.5% of the eligible electorate.

Counts:
- immigrants eligible to vote: 391,040
- Norwegian-born with immigrant parents eligible to vote: 75,860
- combined eligible immigrant-origin electorate: 466,900

Turnout:
- immigrants: 56%
- Norwegian-born with immigrant parents: 60%
- voters without immigrant background: 83%

Approximate votes cast:
- immigrants: ~218,982
- Norwegian-born with immigrant parents: ~45,516
- combined immigrant-origin votes cast: ~264,498

This is an estimate obtained by applying the reported turnout rates to the eligible-population counts.

## 3. Descriptive bloc comparison

For this module only, a descriptive comparison is made between:

Left/green grouping:
Rødt + SV + Ap + Sp + MDG

Right/centre-right grouping:
KrF + Venstre + Høyre + Frp

This grouping is used only to describe vote-direction differences. It is not a claim about formal coalition membership or government formation.

Estimated immigrant-origin distribution:
- left/green grouping: ~59.3%
- right/centre-right grouping: ~35.7%

Among Norwegian-born voters with two Norwegian-born parents, SSB reports approximately:
- left/green grouping: ~48.1%
- right/centre-right grouping: ~47.7%

The immigrant-origin electorate therefore had a left-minus-right descriptive margin about 23 percentage points larger than the non-immigrant-background reference group.

Applied to ~264,500 estimated votes cast, this corresponds to roughly **60,000 additional net left-minus-right votes** relative to a counterfactual in which immigrant-origin voters had voted like the non-immigrant-background reference group.

This is a descriptive standardisation, not a causal estimate.

## 4. What changed from 2021

SSB reports:

- Frp gained about 9 percentage points among immigrants overall.
- Ap lost about 5 points among immigrants overall.
- Among voters with Asian background, Ap fell about 10 points while Frp gained about 12.
- Among African-background immigrants, Ap increased while Frp remained very weak.
- Among immigrants from newer EU member states in Eastern Europe, Frp reached about 42%.
- Among immigrants from Europe outside the EU/EFTA/UK, Frp reached about 31%.

The immigrant-origin electorate therefore became more internally heterogeneous in 2025.

This matters because the aggregate left advantage remained, but the mechanism underneath it changed:
- some origin groups remained strongly left-aligned;
- others moved sharply right;
- turnout increased materially;
- younger Norwegian-born voters with immigrant parents continued to show strong SV/Rødt support.

## 5. Outcome relevance

The current Storting composition for 2025–2029 is:

- Ap 53
- Frp 47
- Høyre 24
- SV 9
- Sp 9
- Rødt 9
- MDG 8
- KrF 7
- Venstre 3

Because Norwegian parliamentary seats are allocated through 19 constituencies plus national leveling seats, a national net-vote estimate cannot be directly translated into a seat count.

Therefore the strongest defensible conclusion is:

**DDR7 — OUTCOME RELEVANCE: MATERIAL, SEAT EFFECT NOT IDENTIFIED**

Rationale:
A roughly 264,500-vote immigrant-origin electorate with a clear aggregate left/green tilt is large enough to affect national party vote shares and potentially marginal constituency/leveling-seat outcomes. But the available analysis does not identify how many seats would have changed under a different demographic voting pattern.

## 6. Comparison with Sweden 2026

Sweden:
- newly naturalized post-2022 citizens alone were estimated by SVT to contribute roughly 30,000–35,000 net centre-left votes;
- that estimate was large relative to the approximately 50,000-vote preliminary bloc margin.

Norway:
- the broader immigrant-origin electorate cast roughly 264,500 votes;
- its estimated left-minus-right margin was materially larger than among voters without immigrant background;
- but the 2025 parliamentary outcome was less narrowly balanced at the national-seat level, and district allocation prevents a simple "decisive vote" estimate.

The countries therefore differ:

> Sweden 2026 presents a narrow-margin outcome-relevance case.  
> Norway 2025 presents a larger structural electorate-alignment case with growing internal fragmentation.

## 7. Language lock

Allowed:
> Voters with immigrant background made a materially large contribution to the left/green vote total in Norway in 2025.

Allowed:
> The immigrant-origin electorate remained more left-leaning than voters without immigrant background, even as Frp made substantial gains in several origin groups.

Do not write:
> Immigrant voters decided the Norwegian election.

Do not write:
> Immigration caused the 2025 parliamentary majority.

The present evidence does not identify either causal claim.

## 8. DDR-01 implication

Norway strengthens two findings simultaneously:

**DDR-CF02 — Left alignment exists but is not stable.**  
Supported: the aggregate left advantage remains, while Frp growth shows weakening bloc uniformity.

**DDR-CF04 — Turnout can matter as much as preference.**  
Supported: 11.5% of the eligible electorate translates into a smaller share of actual votes because turnout among immigrant-origin voters remains much lower than among voters without immigrant background.
