# DDR-01 Germany 2025 + Great Britain 2024 — Comparative Outcome-Relevance Pass v0.1

Status: PROVISIONAL DESCRIPTIVE ANALYSIS  
Parent module: DDR-01 // Demographic Voting Alignment  
Core MIS framework: UNCHANGED

## Purpose

This pass tests whether voters with migration/minority backgrounds were electorally material in Germany's 2025 Bundestag election and Great Britain's 2024 general election.

The two countries are intentionally not forced into one definition:

- Germany: *Einwanderungsgeschichte / Migrationshintergrund* and citizenship determine federal-election eligibility.
- Great Britain: available high-quality election research primarily uses ethnic-minority categories rather than immigrant origin.

The module therefore compares mechanisms, not raw percentages as if the categories were identical.

---

# Germany — 2025

## 1. Electoral scale

Germany expected at least 59.2 million people to be eligible for the 2025 Bundestag election.

The latest pre-election Destatis estimate based on 2023 microcensus data found:
- 17.1 million adults had an immigration history;
- 7.1 million of them would have been eligible to vote in a Bundestag election;
- this represented about 12% of all eligible voters;
- the equivalent share was about 9% in 2013.

Important:
The 7.1 million figure is an approximate 2023 benchmark, not an exact 2025 electoral-register count.

## 2. Voting alignment

The 2025 Konrad Adenauer Foundation study repeated a research design used since 2015 and found substantial changes over the previous five years.

Broad result:
- CDU/CSU was the strongest force among Germans both with and without migration background in the representative survey.
- The migrant-origin electorate was not a uniform left bloc.

Origin-specific result:
- respondents of Turkish origin: SPD clearly strongest; Left above average.
- respondents of Russian origin: SPD clearly strongest; Left above average.
- respondents of Polish origin: AfD strongest, followed by CDU/CSU.
- ethnic-German late repatriates (*Spätaussiedler*): AfD strongest, followed by CDU/CSU.

KAS also found that voting behaviour differed materially by origin and had changed significantly over time.

## 3. Outcome relevance

A potential electorate on the order of 12% is structurally large enough to matter nationally, but the current evidence does not permit a defensible estimate of a single "migration-background net vote" for left versus right.

Why:
- origin groups point in different partisan directions;
- "migration background" includes very different migration histories;
- KAS reports representative survey/voting-intention patterns rather than a validated-voter census of the 2025 ballot;
- German coalition formation is multi-party and cannot be reduced cleanly to a two-bloc counterfactual.

Therefore:

**DDR7 — GERMANY 2025: MATERIAL ELECTORATE / NET NATIONAL DIRECTION NOT IDENTIFIED**

This is a stronger finding than "insufficient data": the electorate is large and subgroup alignment is documented, but aggregation would conceal the central fact that origin groups diverge.

## 4. Germany's key contribution to DDR-01

Germany is currently the clearest counterexample to a generic claim that immigrant-origin voters form a stable left electoral bloc.

The more accurate mechanism is:

> Immigration-origin status matters politically, but origin composition matters more than the broad category alone.

That is analytically important because demographic change can produce different electoral effects depending on which origin groups grow, naturalize and turn out.

# Great Britain — 2024

## 1. Category and scale

The strongest 2024 election evidence is organised by ethnicity rather than immigrant origin.

House of Commons Library estimates that about 16% of the UK population in 2024/25 was from a minority ethnic background. This is a population measure, not a direct estimate of the eligible electorate or turnout.

The Focaldata / UK in a Changing Europe post-election project provides a large representative survey base and subgroup analysis.

## 2. Broad partisan alignment

2024 ethnic-minority vote:
- Labour: 49%
- Conservatives: 20%

Broader ideological grouping reported by Focaldata:
- Labour + Greens + Liberal Democrats: 66%
- Conservatives + Reform UK: 26%

For comparison, among white voters Labour's lead over the Conservatives was much narrower:
- Labour: 33%
- Conservatives: 25%

This confirms that ethnic-minority voters remained substantially more Labour/left-liberal leaning in aggregate.

## 3. Historical trajectory

Available election-study series show Labour has led among ethnic-minority voters throughout the modern comparable period.

Selected historical Labour vs Conservative shares among ethnic-minority voters:
- 2010: Labour 60%, Conservative 16%
- 2015: Labour 65%, Conservative 23%
- 2017: Labour 73%, Conservative 39%
- 2019: Labour 64%, Conservative 20%
- 2024: Labour 49%, Conservative 20%

Methodological note:
These estimates come from different election-study/survey waves and should be treated as a trajectory rather than exact like-for-like panel measurements.

The central 2024 development is therefore not disappearance of Labour's advantage; it is fragmentation within a still strongly Labour-leaning electorate.

## 4. Internal fragmentation in 2024

Focaldata found:
- Labour fell by 13 points among Asian voters to 43%;
- the Greens gained substantially among Asian voters;
- independent/minor-party support also rose;
- Labour's vote share collapsed by about 28 points among Muslim voters;
- five of the seven constituencies Labour lost had Muslim populations above 25%;
- Leicester East, the only Conservative gain, has the highest Hindu population in the country;
- Conservative performance was relatively stronger in several Hindu-heavy seats.

This is direct evidence that "ethnic minority" is not one political bloc.

## 5. Outcome relevance

Nationally, the 2024 Labour victory was large enough that current evidence does not support describing ethnic-minority voting as uniquely decisive to government formation.

At constituency level, however, subgroup realignment was demonstrably material:
- concentrated Muslim-voter movement was associated with major Labour losses;
- five of Labour's seven lost seats were in constituencies with Muslim population shares over 25%;
- independents and smaller parties benefited from issue-specific shifts in several highly diverse seats.

Therefore:

**DDR7 — GREAT BRITAIN 2024: MATERIAL NATIONAL ELECTORATE / LOCALLY DECISIVE PATTERNS / NATIONAL GOVERNMENT EFFECT NOT IDENTIFIED**

"Locally decisive" here refers to constituency-level electoral relevance, not a claim that ethnicity itself causally determined an individual's vote.

## 6. Great Britain's key contribution to DDR-01

The British case shows a two-layer pattern:

1. broad aggregate persistence:
   ethnic-minority voters remained much more Labour/left-liberal than white voters;

2. subgroup fragmentation:
   Muslim, Hindu, Asian and other groups moved differently enough to alter individual constituency outcomes.

This makes Britain closer to Norway than to Sweden in one important sense:

> an old left-aligned demographic coalition remains visible, but internal political differentiation is increasing.

# Germany vs Great Britain

## Common result

Both countries reject the idea of a homogeneous "immigrant/minority vote."

Germany:
- broad migration-background category contains groups leaning strongly toward SPD/Left and groups leaning strongly toward AfD/CDU.

Great Britain:
- ethnic-minority voters remain Labour-heavy overall, but religious and ancestry subgroups moved in sharply different directions in 2024.

## Important difference

Germany:
- **cross-bloc heterogeneity is already structural** at broad origin-group level.

Great Britain:
- **aggregate left/Labour alignment remains strong**, while fragmentation is growing inside that coalition.

This produces a useful comparative typology:

- Sweden 2026: **left-coalition persistence / re-strengthening**
- Norway 2025: **left-coalition fragmentation**
- Great Britain 2024: **left-coalition persistence with local fragmentation**
- Germany 2025: **origin-dependent cross-bloc heterogeneity**

# New provisional findings

## DDR-CF09 — Origin composition can matter more than immigrant-background status

**SUPPORTED — Germany**

A broad migration-background variable can hide opposite partisan alignments among Turkish-, Russian-, Polish-origin and late-repatriate voters.

## DDR-CF10 — Aggregate alignment can coexist with constituency-level defection

**SUPPORTED — Great Britain**

A demographic electorate can remain strongly aligned with one party nationally while subgroup shifts materially alter individual seat outcomes.

## DDR-CF11 — Demographic voting effects should be analysed at more than one geographic scale

**SUPPORTED**

National vote shares can conceal:
- concentrated constituency effects in FPTP systems;
- origin-group divergence in proportional/multi-party systems;
- turnout effects;
- local issue-driven candidate effects.
