## Check 1 — Direct vs indirect mechanism

No contradiction found.

Across the working files, the direct channel remains:

> how immigrant-origin / minority / naturalized electorates themselves participate and vote.

The indirect channel remains:

> how immigration or demographic exposure may change turnout or party choice in the wider electorate.

The two channels are not treated as interchangeable.

## Check 3 — Background vs composition

One wording issue was corrected.

The British long-run multivariate evidence supports an **independent predictive association** between ethnicity and Labour voting after controls. It does not establish ethnicity as a causal treatment.

The Norway panel likewise supports the narrower statement that age, gender and education do not explain away the observed 2019–2023 rightward shift. It should not be phrased as a generic causal "immigrant-origin effect."

Status:
**PASS AFTER WORDING CORRECTION**

## Check 15 — Evidence tiers

Potential ambiguity identified:

Tier A/B/C/D describes evidence quality for a **specific measure**, not necessarily an entire country.

Chile is the clearest example:
- A for electorate composition / turnout;
- open or C-proxy at best for party choice.

France:
- stronger for turnout;
- weaker for party-choice comparability.

Costa Rica:
- A for administrative electorate structure;
- D/open for party choice.

Therefore no future table should assign one single country-wide evidence tier when variable-specific tiers differ materially.

Status:
**METHOD CLARIFICATION — APPLY VARIABLE-SPECIFIC TIERS IN v1.0**
