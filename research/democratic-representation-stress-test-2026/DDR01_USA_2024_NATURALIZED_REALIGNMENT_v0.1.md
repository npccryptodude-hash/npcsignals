# DDR-01 United States 2024 — Naturalized-Citizen Realignment v0.1

Status: PROVISIONAL DESCRIPTIVE ANALYSIS  
Parent module: DDR-01 // Demographic Voting Alignment  
Core MIS framework: UNCHANGED

## Purpose

This pass tests how naturalized U.S. citizens voted in the 2024 presidential election, how that differed from 2020, and whether the change appears to reflect persuasion or turnout composition.

The analysis is based primarily on Pew Research Center's validated-voter study and Census-based electorate estimates.

---

## 1. Electorate size

Pew Research Center estimated that about 23.8 million naturalized citizens were eligible to vote in 2022, roughly 10% of the U.S. eligible electorate.

In Pew's validated-voter study of the 2024 election, naturalized citizens made up about 9% of actual voters.

Interpretation:
Naturalized citizens constitute a nationally material voting bloc, but their share of actual voters is slightly below their share of the eligible electorate.

---

## 2. Vote choice: 2020 vs 2024

Among validated naturalized-citizen voters:

2020:
- Biden: 59%
- Trump: 38%

2024:
- Harris: 51%
- Trump: 47%

Net Democratic advantage:
- 2020: +21 percentage points
- 2024: +4 percentage points

Change:
- Democratic advantage narrowed by about 17 percentage points.

This is one of the clearest documented shifts in DDR-01 so far.

---

## 3. Was this mainly vote-switching?

Pew's panel evidence indicates that the shift was driven primarily by turnout composition rather than mass individual defection.

Key findings:
- about 22% of naturalized citizens who voted in 2024 had not voted in 2020;
- 57% of this 2024-only voter group voted for Trump;
- among naturalized citizens who voted in 2020 but did not vote in 2024, 67% had voted for Biden;
- direct Biden-to-Trump switching existed, but Pew found that it was roughly offset by Trump-to-Harris switching.

Interpretation:

> The aggregate realignment among naturalized citizens was produced more by who entered and left the electorate than by a large-scale conversion of the same voters from one party to the other.

This is a strong example of DDR-CF04: turnout composition can matter as much as vote preference.

---

## 4. Subgroup movement

Pew reports Trump gains across several naturalized-citizen racial/ethnic groups:

White naturalized citizens:
- Trump 2020: 41%
- Trump 2024: 55%

Hispanic naturalized citizens:
- Trump 2020: 39%
- Trump 2024: 51%

Asian naturalized citizens:
- Trump 2020: 35%
- Trump 2024: 46%

These movements point in the same overall direction, but they do not mean all naturalized citizens became Republican-leaning.

The aggregate 2024 result remained nearly even:
- Harris 51%
- Trump 47%

---

## 5. Outcome relevance

Pew estimates naturalized citizens were about 9% of actual 2024 voters.

Because the group split almost evenly, the naturalized-citizen electorate did not provide a large net national vote advantage to either major candidate in 2024.

However, the shift from a +21-point Democratic advantage in 2020 to +4 in 2024 was politically material because it removed much of a previously substantial Democratic margin within a large electorate.

This distinction matters:

- 2020: naturalized citizens were a clear Democratic-leaning electorate.
- 2024: naturalized citizens were close to evenly divided.

Therefore:

**DDR7 — UNITED STATES 2024: MATERIAL REALIGNMENT / LIMITED NET NATIONAL BLOC ADVANTAGE**

The central effect is not that naturalized citizens "won the election" for either side, but that a large electorate ceased to provide the same partisan advantage it had four years earlier.

---

## 6. Counterfactual caution

A simple counterfactual such as "if naturalized citizens had voted as in 2020, the national result would have changed" is not justified without state-level allocation and turnout modelling.

Reasons:
- the U.S. president is elected through the Electoral College, not the national popular vote;
- naturalized-citizen shares vary widely by state;
- turnout and vote choice differed across demographic subgroups;
- national survey percentages cannot be directly translated into exact state vote totals.

A valid electoral-impact model would need:
1. naturalized-citizen eligible electorate by state;
2. 2024 turnout by state;
3. state-specific vote choice;
4. comparison with state margins;
5. uncertainty intervals.

---

## 7. Geographic relevance

Pew's Census-based analysis shows naturalized citizens are unevenly distributed across states.

Highest eligible-electorate shares include:
- California: ~21%
- New Jersey: ~19%
- New York: ~19%
- Florida: ~17%

Naturalized-citizen shares were also non-trivial in several closely watched states, but the national DDR-01 module should not infer state-level decisiveness without state-specific validated vote-choice evidence.

---

## 8. US contribution to DDR-01

### DDR-CF12 — Demographic realignment can occur through turnout composition rather than persuasion

**SUPPORTED**

The U.S. case is unusually strong because Pew's panel design directly distinguishes:
- voters who switched candidates;
- voters who entered the electorate;
- voters who dropped out.

The 2020→2024 naturalized-citizen shift was driven mainly by the latter two.

### DDR-CF13 — A demographic group can remain electorally large while losing partisan distinctiveness

**SUPPORTED**

Naturalized citizens remained about one-tenth of the eligible electorate, but their presidential vote moved from a clear Democratic tilt to near parity.

### DDR-CF14 — Outcome relevance is not the same as net advantage

**SUPPORTED**

A group can matter structurally even when its aggregate vote is nearly balanced. The relevant political effect may be the disappearance of a previous partisan advantage rather than the creation of a new one.

## 9. Comparison with current DDR-01 cases

Sweden 2026:
- strong centre-left alignment
- estimated new-citizen net contribution large relative to narrow bloc margin

Norway 2025:
- left advantage persists
- Frp gains indicate fragmentation
- seat effect not identified

Great Britain 2024:
- Labour/left-liberal aggregate alignment persists
- strong local subgroup fragmentation

Germany 2025:
- no single national migration-background direction
- origin groups point toward different parties

United States 2024:
- previous Democratic advantage largely collapsed
- naturalized electorate nearly split
- change driven strongly by turnout composition

Provisional typology:

- Sweden: **alignment persistence / re-strengthening**
- Norway: **alignment fragmentation**
- Great Britain: **aggregate persistence + local fragmentation**
- Germany: **origin-dependent cross-bloc heterogeneity**
- United States: **rapid partisan convergence via turnout composition**
