NPCsignals — Democratic Representation Stress Test 2026

This directory contains the public audit materials for the NPCsignals Democratic Representation Stress Test 2026.

The project does not rank countries by a single democracy score. It tests how voter preference, political voice, governing power, institutional restraint, information integrity, trust, and political realignment interact across a fixed 15-country sample.

Six dimensions

I — Institutional Democracy
Formal democratic institutions, elections, parliament, rule of law, rights, and institutional capacity.

II — Political Voice / Representation
Whether citizens believe people like them have influence, whether consultation is responsive, whether changing preferences translate into representation, and whether perceived voice is improving or deteriorating.

III — Mandate Integrity
Whether major political decisions are supported by sufficient mandate depth, public alignment, procedural inclusion, legislative depth, institutional restraint, reversibility, and mandate-persistence legitimacy.

IV — Information Integrity
Media independence, covert partisan communication, coordinated foreign influence, persistent corrected falsehoods, political pressure on public-service media, legal restrictions on political expression, and corrective capacity.

V — Trust & Legitimacy
Trust in government, parliament, political parties, courts, and rule-of-law institutions, kept separate from support for democracy as a governing principle.

VI — Polarization / Realignment
Electoral realignment, challenger growth, governing translation, and delegitimizing polarization.

Mandate Integrity case design

Each country receives six contemporary cases using a predetermined composition:

2 × Electoral / Power Translation
2 × Major Policy / Fiscal / Social
2 × Institutional / High Persistence

This design replaced an earlier three-case pilot after case-selection stress testing showed that unbalanced samples could materially distort country-level results.

Mandate Integrity variables

Each case is scored from 0 to 4 on seven variables:

Mandate strength — 20%
Public alignment — 15%
Pre-legislative inclusion — 10%
Legislative depth — 15%
Institutional restraint — 20%
Reversibility safeguard — 10%
Mandate-persistence legitimacy — 10%

Provisional output bands:

85–100 HEALTHY
70–84 WATCH
50–69 STRESS
0–49 FAILURE

These are case-based stress bands, not a general democracy ranking.

Core principles

The lower the reversibility of a political decision, the stronger and more specific the democratic mandate should be.

A mandate grants temporary governing authority, not permanent ownership of the state.

A mandate for an objective is not automatically a mandate for every institutional mechanism used to achieve it.

Institutional opposition is not mandate override when the opposing institution possesses its own valid democratic or constitutional mandate.

Executive ambition is not democratic erosion when the system retains the capacity to say no.

Coalition exclusion is not itself mandate override.

Political realignment cannot itself lower democratic classification.

Primary candidate

The institutional mechanisms that translate voter preference into governing power are often long-standing. Their democratic consequences become more visible when electoral realignment grows large enough to stress those mechanisms.

The project currently distinguishes four broad outcomes:

ABSORPTION — changing preferences translate into governing influence while ordinary restraints remain effective.

DELAYED ABSORPTION — transmission is initially distorted or delayed, but eventual turnover occurs through ordinary democratic mechanisms.

BOTTLENECK — representation increases substantially while governing translation remains much weaker.

OVERRIDE / RESTRAINT STRESS — either electoral mandate ceases to reliably determine who can govern, or elected power increasingly attempts to escape ordinary democratic restraint.

Important limitation

The project is exploratory and case-based. It is designed as a stress instrument and longitudinal archive, not as a definitive democracy index. Cross-country causal claims remain provisional unless explicitly marked as supported.
