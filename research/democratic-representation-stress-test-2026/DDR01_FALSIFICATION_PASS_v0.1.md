# DDR-01 Falsification Pass v0.1

Status: COMPLETED FIRST FALSIFICATION PASS  
Method status: FROZEN v0.1  
Evidence status: OPEN  
Signal status: NOT A SIGNAL

## Purpose

This pass does not ask whether the current DDR-01 model can be supported.

It asks where the model breaks.

The working model frozen before this pass is:

> Demographic change can alter electoral competition through at least two distinct channels: the direct voting behaviour of immigrant-origin/minority electorates and indirect responses elsewhere in the electorate. The strength and direction of both channels vary by origin composition, turnout, geography, party system and time.

The purpose of this document is to test weaker, more specific claims that could easily be overgeneralized from the evidence.

---

## FALSIFICATION TEST 1

### Claim tested
"Immigrant-origin electorates generally form a stable left-of-centre voting bloc."

### Evidence against

#### Germany
The 2025 KAS migration-background study shows sharply different party preferences by origin:
- Turkish-origin respondents: SPD clearly strongest; Left above average.
- Russian-origin respondents: SPD clearly strongest; Left above average.
- Polish-origin respondents: AfD strongest, followed by CDU/CSU.
- ethnic-German late repatriates: AfD strongest, followed by CDU/CSU.

#### United States
Naturalized citizens moved from Biden 59 / Trump 38 in 2020 to Harris 51 / Trump 47 in 2024.

The Democratic advantage collapsed from +21 to +4 percentage points.

#### Norway
The aggregate immigrant-origin electorate remained left-leaning in 2025, but Frp gained materially, particularly among several Asian and Eastern European origin groups.

### Result

**FALSIFIED AS A GENERAL RULE**

A broad left alignment exists in some countries and periods, but it is neither universal nor stable.

### Surviving formulation

> Some immigrant-origin electorates show persistent left-of-centre aggregate alignment, but origin composition and electoral realignment can substantially weaken or reverse that relationship.

---

## FALSIFICATION TEST 2

### Claim tested
"More immigration or ethnic diversity pushes the wider electorate to the right."

### Evidence against

#### Denmark — urban exception
The quasi-random refugee-allocation study finds that higher refugee exposure increased support for anti-immigration right parties in most municipalities, but in the largest urban municipalities the effect was absent or, if anything, reversed.

This is not a small technical exception. It means the direction of the mechanism changes with local context.

#### Italy — 2022
A 2025 spatial analysis of nearly all Italian municipalities in the 2022 national election finds:
- no significant relationship, or negative correlations, between immigrant presence and Lega/FdI support across most of Italy;
- especially weak/negative relationships in major urban areas;
- a positive association for FdI concentrated in Southern Italy.

### Result

**FALSIFIED AS A UNIVERSAL CURRENT RULE**

Historical rightward counter-mobilization is real in credible causal studies, but it cannot be projected mechanically across place or time.

### Surviving formulation

> Immigration exposure can produce rightward counter-mobilization under some conditions, but the effect is geographically and temporally conditional and may weaken, disappear or reverse.

---

## FALSIFICATION TEST 3

### Claim tested
"Demographic electoral change mainly occurs because existing voters switch parties."

### Evidence against

#### United States
Pew's validated-voter panel shows that the major 2020→2024 shift among naturalized citizens was driven primarily by turnout composition.

- 22% of naturalized citizens who voted in 2024 had not voted in 2020.
- 57% of this group voted Trump.
- Among 2020 naturalized voters who did not vote in 2024, 67% had voted Biden.
- Direct Biden→Trump switching was roughly offset by Trump→Harris switching.

### Result

**FALSIFIED**

Aggregate demographic realignment can occur without mass persuasion.

### Surviving formulation

> Demographic realignment can be generated by changes in who participates, even when individual switching is limited.

---

## FALSIFICATION TEST 4

### Claim tested
"If a demographic electorate is strongly aligned with one bloc nationally, it should have similar effects across constituencies."

### Evidence against

#### Great Britain
In 2024, ethnic-minority voters remained much more Labour/left-liberal in aggregate than white voters.

At the same time:
- Labour support fell sharply among Muslim voters;
- several high-Muslim constituencies produced major Labour losses;
- Hindu-heavy areas showed different patterns;
- Asian voters fragmented toward Greens, independents and other parties.

### Result

**FALSIFIED**

National aggregate alignment can coexist with local electoral defection and opposite subgroup movement.

### Surviving formulation

> Demographic alignment must be analysed at national, local and subgroup levels simultaneously.

---

## FALSIFICATION TEST 5

### Claim tested
"A growing immigrant-origin share of the electorate translates proportionally into political influence."

### Evidence against

#### Norway
The immigrant-origin eligible electorate expanded sharply from 2005 to 2025, but turnout remained far below the non-immigrant-background reference group.

2025 turnout:
- immigrants: 56%
- Norwegian-born with immigrant parents: 60%
- voters without immigrant background: 83%

#### United States
Naturalized citizens were about 10% of the eligible electorate in the latest pre-election Census benchmark but about 9% of actual 2024 voters.

### Result

**FALSIFIED**

Population/electorate growth alone is an inadequate measure of political weight.

### Surviving formulation

> Electoral influence depends on eligible share × turnout × vote distribution, not demographic share alone.

---

## FALSIFICATION TEST 6

### Claim tested
"A materially large demographic vote contribution means that group decided the election."

### Evidence against

#### Sweden 2026
SVT's estimate for post-2022 new citizens was roughly 30,000–35,000 net centre-left votes under stated assumptions.

The preliminary national bloc margin was of the same order of magnitude.

However:
- turnout for the exact subgroup was assumed, not directly observed;
- broader origin-group Valu estimates were applied to new citizens;
- age adjustment was approximate;
- other voter groups can independently have net contributions larger than a close margin;
- no unique counterfactual identifies this group as the singular cause of the outcome.

### Result

**FALSIFIED AS AN INFERENCE RULE**

Materiality does not establish unique causation.

### Surviving formulation

> A demographic electorate may be outcome-relevant without being uniquely outcome-determinative.

---

## FALSIFICATION TEST 7

### Claim tested
"Immigrant-origin status itself is the main explanatory variable."

### Evidence against

Current evidence repeatedly shows competing variables:

Germany:
- origin group radically changes partisan alignment.

Great Britain:
- religion, ancestry, geography and issue salience alter subgroup voting.

Norway:
- region of origin and generation matter strongly.

United States:
- turnout composition and race/ethnicity matter.

Denmark:
- urban/rural context changes the sign of the refugee-exposure effect.

Italy:
- geography, education and employment materially condition the association.

### Result

**PARTIALLY FALSIFIED / MAJOR CONFOUNDING RISK**

Broad immigrant-origin status has descriptive value, but it is often too coarse to explain the mechanism.

### Surviving formulation

> Immigrant-origin status is best treated as a top-level grouping variable that must be decomposed by origin, generation, turnout, geography and other relevant covariates whenever data allow.

---

# What survived the falsification pass?

The strongest claim is not:

> Immigrants vote left.

It is not:

> Immigration moves the native electorate right.

It is not:

> Demographic change decides close elections.

What survives is a narrower mechanism model:

> Demographic change can alter electoral competition through both direct voting and indirect electorate responses. Neither channel has a fixed ideological direction. Their magnitude and direction depend on turnout, origin composition, geography, party supply and time.

This formulation survives every current counterexample because the counterexamples are part of the mechanism rather than exceptions hidden from it.

---

# Model stress status

### Survives strongly
- Direct and indirect channels must be separated.
- Turnout must be treated independently from preference.
- Origin heterogeneity is analytically necessary.
- Outcome relevance must be separated from causal decisiveness.
- Geographic scale matters.

### Weakened
- Any broad expectation of persistent left alignment.
- Any broad expectation of rightward counter-mobilization.
- Any assumption that immigrant-origin status alone explains the pattern.

### Rejected
- "Immigrants generally vote left" as a universal rule.
- "Immigration generally moves voters right" as a universal rule.
- "A demographic contribution larger than the winning margin proves that group decided the election."
- "Demographic realignment mainly means voters switching sides."

- income/class;
- urbanization;
- religion;
- origin country/region;
- generation;
- party-system structure.

If broad immigrant-origin status largely disappears after those controls, DDR-01 should be reframed from a demographic-alignment model into a compositional-electorate model.
