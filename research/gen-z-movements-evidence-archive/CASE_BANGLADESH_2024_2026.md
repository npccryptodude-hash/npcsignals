# bangladesh 2024–2026

## short answer

bangladesh demonstrates that a youth-led uprising can help remove a long-serving government without the protest movement subsequently dominating electoral politics.

## trigger and escalation

student protests began around the public-sector quota system and widened into a broader movement against the government.

on 5 august 2024, prime minister sheikh hasina left the country. the army announced her resignation and plans for an interim government. student leaders demanded that a civilian interim government be formed in consultation with them.

## human-rights record

**CONFIRMED**

ohchr documented serious allegations of disproportionate and unlawful force during the protests, including the use of live ammunition. its preliminary analysis reported hundreds of deaths and large-scale arrests.

ohchr's later reporting describes the protests as rooted in broader grievances about governance, corruption and access to economic, social and cultural rights, and states that the repression involved serious and systematic human-rights violations.

## political outcome

**CONFIRMED**

in the february 2026 parliamentary election:

- the bangladesh nationalist party won a large parliamentary majority;
- the youth-led national citizen party, formed out of the protest movement, won only a small number of seats.

reuters described the result as demonstrating the difficulty of converting youth-led uprising power into electoral power.

## interpretation

**SUPPORTED**

the uprising succeeded at regime removal but did not give the student movement equivalent control over the post-uprising political order.

## analytical path

mobilization → regime collapse → transitional reform → election → established political actors regain dominance
