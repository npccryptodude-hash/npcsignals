# gen z movements evidence archive

status: **v0.4 / public research archive**

last updated: **2026-11-08**

## purpose

this archive documents youth- and student-led protest movements and their political outcomes.

the goal is not to prove that gen z movements share one ideology, one organization or one outcome. the goal is to preserve a source-traceable record of what happened, what can be verified, what remains disputed, and how different movements developed over time.

the archive is designed so that students, teachers, researchers and other readers can:

- reconstruct a case chronologically;
- distinguish verified facts from interpretation;
- inspect the sources behind a claim;
- compare different movements without assuming they are identical;
- see what happened after the visible protest phase ended.

for a lighter entry point, open [INDEX.md](./INDEX.md).

## evidence labels

**CONFIRMED** — supported by primary documentation or multiple strong independent sources.

**SUPPORTED** — credible evidence supports the claim, but the available record is incomplete.

**CONTESTED** — credible actors or sources disagree materially.

**UNRESOLVED** — evidence is insufficient to reach a conclusion.

**NOT ESTABLISHED** — a claim has been investigated but is not supported by the available record.

## core analytical distinction

this archive separates four forms of power:

1. **mobilization power** — ability to bring people into collective action.
2. **organizational power** — ability to sustain structures, leadership, logistics and decision-making.
3. **electoral power** — ability to convert support into votes and representation.
4. **institutional power** — ability to govern, reform institutions and sustain change.

these should not be treated as interchangeable.

## source policy

medium articles and social posts are not treated as evidence simply because npcsignals published them.

claims in this archive should trace back to original documents, official records, court material, election authorities, established monitoring organizations, international organizations, or strong independent journalism.

## visual policy

visual diagrams in this archive are summaries of documented relationships and chronology. they do not create new evidence.

where a diagram simplifies a disputed or unresolved point, the case file and evidence ledger control.

## editorial principle

connections are not identities. visibility is not verification. mobilization is not institutional change.
