# master timeline

updated: **2026-11-08**

## thailand

**2020** — youth- and student-led pro-democracy protests expand demands to include constitutional reform and reform of the monarchy.

**november 2020 onward** — thai authorities resume intensive use of lèse-majesté prosecutions against democracy activists.

**2024–2026** — prominent activists remain imprisoned, prosecuted or denied bail; long-tail legal consequences continue years after the mass mobilization.

**24 february 2026** — arnon nampa and three other democracy activists receive new section 112 convictions in a case tied to a 2020 rally.

## bangladesh

**july 2024** — student protests grow from opposition to the public-sector quota system into a broader anti-government movement.

**5 august 2024** — prime minister sheikh hasina leaves the country; an interim-government process follows.

**2024–2025** — accountability and institutional reform become central post-uprising issues.

**12 february 2026** — parliamentary election held. bnp wins a large majority; the youth-led national citizen party wins limited representation.

**17 february 2026** — tarique rahman sworn in as prime minister.

**7 september 2026** — parliament passes legislation on enforced disappearance and a new national human rights commission framework.

## serbia

**november 2024** — collapse of the novi sad railway-station canopy kills 16 people and triggers sustained protests focused on accountability and corruption.

**2024–2026** — student networks become the central organizing force in a wider national movement.

**25 october 2026** — parliamentary election.

**6 november 2026** — final result certified. the students' list wins 140 of 250 seats, clearing the way for a student-movement-led government.

## france

**september 2026** — high-school protests begin around teacher shortages, overcrowding, deteriorating facilities and other school conditions.

**1 october 2026** — more than 1,020 high schools are affected by strikes or protests, according to the education ministry.

**6–16 october 2026** — the movement broadens; university students, parents, teachers, unions and older protest networks increasingly appear.

**26 october 2026** — government announces measures on parcoursup, class sizes, staffing and assessment.

**2 november 2026** — schools reopen after midterm break; blockades resume at reduced scale.

**5 november 2026** — major cross-sector strike and protest day links students with national union mobilization.

**7 november 2026** — school-level protests persist, with growing tension involving parents, staff, school leaders and police.

**status 8 november 2026** — france remains a live case. the movement has won policy concessions but its long-term political and organizational outcome is unresolved.
