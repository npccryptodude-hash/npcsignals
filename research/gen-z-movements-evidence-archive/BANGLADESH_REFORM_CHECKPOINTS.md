# bangladesh — reform checkpoints

version: **0.4**

status date: **8 november 2026**

purpose: distinguish regime change, legal enactment and actual institutional implementation.

## checkpoint 01 — regime transition

**status: PASS / CONFIRMED**

5 august 2024:
- sheikh hasina leaves the country;
- interim-government process begins;
- student leaders become important actors in the transition debate.

## checkpoint 02 — reform architecture

**status: PASS / CONFIRMED**

the interim government establishes commissions covering:
- constitutional reform;
- elections;
- judiciary;
- policing;
- public administration;
- anti-corruption.

their reports feed into the national-consensus process and July Charter.

## checkpoint 03 — July Charter

**status: CONFIRMED / CONTESTED IMPLEMENTATION**

the charter contains 84 reform proposals, including 47 requiring constitutional amendment.

the charter receives majority support in the referendum held alongside the 2026 election.

important qualification:
- parties disagreed on implementation;
- NCP did not sign the charter before the election;
- post-election implementation remains politically contested.

## checkpoint 04 — peaceful elected transfer

**status: PASS / CONFIRMED**

12 february 2026:
- parliamentary election held.

17 february 2026:
- tarique rahman sworn in as prime minister.

the elected BNP government replaces the interim administration.

## checkpoint 05 — legal protection for uprising participants

**status: PASS / CONFIRMED**

8 april 2026:
parliament passes the July Mass Uprising (Protection and Liability Determination) law.

reported effects:
- civil and criminal cases relating to participation in the uprising are to be withdrawn;
- new cases over participation are prohibited;
- murder allegations remain subject to investigation.

## checkpoint 06 — interim ordinances

**status: PARTIAL**

the elected government reviews 133 interim-government ordinances.

reported outcome:
- 98 retained unchanged as laws;
- 15 retained in revised form;
- 20 repealed or allowed to lapse.

this demonstrates substantial legal carryover but not full adoption of the interim reform agenda.

## checkpoint 07 — criminal procedure safeguards

**status: PASS / ENACTED; IMPLEMENTATION REQUIRES MONITORING**

changes to the code of criminal procedure strengthen safeguards against arbitrary arrest, including section 54 reforms.

## checkpoint 08 — enforced disappearance law

**status: PASS / ENACTED, QUALITY CONTESTED**

september 2026:
parliament adopts the Enforced Disappearance Prevention and Redress Act.

positive elements reported include:
- enforced disappearance defined as a specific criminal offense;
- victim compensation / rehabilitation framework;
- central database;
- investigation and trial deadlines.

criticism:
- rights groups and opposition actors say the elected-government law weakens protections contained in the interim ordinance;
- investigatory independence remains contested.

## checkpoint 09 — national human rights commission

**status: PASS / ENACTED, INDEPENDENCE CONTESTED**

september 2026:
new NHRC law adopted.

human rights watch argues that the replacement framework weakens:
- direct investigatory power over security agencies;
- political independence;
- appointment safeguards.

therefore:

**law enacted ≠ institutional independence established.**

## checkpoint 10 — police reform

**status: UNRESOLVED / LIMITED**

the independent police-commission proposal associated with interim reform has not been implemented according to sources interviewed by the UK Home Office fact-finding mission.

## checkpoint 11 — judicial reform

**status: UNRESOLVED / PARTIAL REVERSAL**

some interim initiatives, including a separate judicial secretariat, were reported as not carried forward by the elected government.

## checkpoint 12 — anti-corruption reform

**status: UNRESOLVED**

the archive does not yet have sufficient evidence to classify the broader anti-corruption reform agenda as implemented.

## checkpoint 13 — civic space

**status: SUPPORTED IMPROVEMENT / MIXED**

fact-finding sources describe:
- wider room for political parties, journalists and civil society than under the former government;
- sharp reduction in enforced disappearances compared with the prior period;
- continued concerns over detention, political violence, mob violence and institutional weakness.

## overall classification

**regime change: CONFIRMED**

**electoral transition: CONFIRMED**

**reform carryover: CONFIRMED / PARTIAL**

**durable institutional transformation: UNRESOLVED**

## analytical significance

bangladesh shows why "the government fell" is not an adequate measure of movement success.

the relevant sequence is:

uprising → transition → reform design → election → legislative carryover / reversal → implementation test.
