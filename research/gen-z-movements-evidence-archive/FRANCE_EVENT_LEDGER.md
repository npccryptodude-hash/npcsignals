# france 2026 — event ledger

version: **0.3**

scope: chronology of the high-school-led movement and its expansion into a broader social movement.

## september

### 18 september
**event:** students at Antoine-de-Saint-Exupéry high school in Créteil organize in a Snapchat group following a teachers' strike.  
**significance:** earliest documented digital coordination at the school later identified as the movement's starting point.  
**status:** CONFIRMED  
**source:** Le Monde, 31 October 2026.

### 21 september
**event:** protest activity at Saint-Exupéry high school becomes the visible starting point of the wider movement.  
**grievances:** missing teachers and staff, dilapidated facilities, schedules, overcrowding and Parcoursup stress.  
**status:** CONFIRMED  
**source:** Le Monde, 29 September 2026.

### 25 september
**event:** police memo records calls to block high schools circulating on Snapchat and Instagram.  
**assessment:** calls were described as disorganized and not clearly linked to student unions.  
**status:** CONFIRMED AS POLICE ASSESSMENT  
**source:** Le Monde, 31 October 2026.

### 28 september
**event:** 180 schools affected nationwide; movement expands beyond the Paris region.  
**status:** CONFIRMED  
**source:** Interior Ministry figure reported by Le Monde.

### 29 september
**event:** USL calls for blockades "in all high schools" and says it is expanding what began in the Paris region because the grievances are national.  
**significance:** clear transition from local replication to national aggregation.  
**status:** CONFIRMED  
**source:** Le Monde / AFP.

## october

### 1 october
**event:** more than 1,020 high schools affected, including 222 blockades.  
**scale:** approximately one in four high schools nationwide.  
**status:** CONFIRMED  
**source:** Education Ministry figure reported by Le Monde.

### 6 october
**event:** university participation becomes visibly established, including campus blockades.  
**status:** CONFIRMED  
**source:** Le Monde reporting on university mobilization.

### 8 october
**event:** growing scrutiny of police intervention, arrests and force used around school blockades.  
**significance:** state response becomes part of the movement's mobilizing narrative.  
**status:** CONFIRMED  
**source:** Le Monde, 8 October 2026.

### 12 october
**event:** school leaders report exhaustion, damage and violent incidents at a minority of schools.  
**documented context:** Education Ministry says at least 24 high schools had been burned or ransacked and several hundred damaged; most schools remained peaceful or outside the movement.  
**status:** CONFIRMED  
**source:** Le Monde, 12 October 2026.

### 13 october
**event:** national action day draws high-school students, university students, parents and teachers.  
**turnout:** 161,500–480,000 nationally depending on source.  
**status:** CONFIRMED / TURNOUT DISPUTED  
**source:** Le Monde, 14 October 2026.

### 14 october
**event:** students from grandes écoles and elite institutions are documented joining the movement.  
**significance:** social and educational coalition broadens beyond the initial lycée base.  
**status:** CONFIRMED  
**source:** Le Monde, 14 October 2026.

### 16 october
**event:** online calls explicitly seek a "convergence of struggles" between high-school students and Yellow Vest networks ahead of 17 October.  
**status:** CONFIRMED  
**source:** Le Monde, 16 October 2026.

### 17 october
**event:** major national protest. organizers report 800,000 participants; Interior Ministry reports 550,000.  
**character:** peaceful mass participation alongside separate violent and looting episodes in several cities.  
**significance:** the protest field expands far beyond the original school grievances.  
**status:** CONFIRMED / TURNOUT DISPUTED  
**source:** Le Monde, 18 October 2026.

### 26 october
**event:** government announces measures responding to student grievances.  
**measures include:** Parcoursup replacement plan, staffing, class-size measures, assessment review and school-condition responses.  
**status:** CONFIRMED  
**source:** Le Monde, 27 October 2026.

### 31 october
**event:** Le Monde reconstructs social-media infrastructure behind the movement and documents bottom-up private group coordination.  
**significance:** strengthens evidence for replication without proving central command.  
**status:** CONFIRMED  
**source:** Le Monde, 31 October 2026.

## november

### 2 november
**event:** schools reopen after the two-week midterm break; blockades resume but below early-October peak.  
**status:** CONFIRMED  
**source:** Le Monde, 7 November 2026.

### 5 november
**event:** nationwide cross-sector strike and demonstrations connect high-school and university movements with major trade unions, workers and Yellow Vest networks.  
**scale:** 193 marches; Interior Ministry estimates 575,000 participants, CGT 1.6 million.  
**status:** CONFIRMED / TURNOUT DISPUTED  
**source:** Le Monde, 5–6 November 2026.

### 7 november
**event:** school-level blockades continue at reduced scale. parents, school leaders and police increasingly contest blockade tactics.  
**paris:** about 30 of 159 high schools affected by blockades or attempted blockades on typical days reported after reopening.  
**staff injuries:** more than 50 education staff injured during the first week back, bringing ministry-reported total to 220 since protests began.  
**status:** CONFIRMED  
**source:** Le Monde, 7 November 2026.

## current reading — 8 november 2026

the chronology supports four distinct stages:

1. **origin** — school-level grievances and local digital coordination;
2. **replication** — rapid spread between schools without initial central command;
3. **aggregation** — student unions and university networks create national interfaces;
4. **coalition expansion** — unions, workers, Yellow Vest networks and other groups broaden the movement beyond education.

the archive does **not** establish that the broader coalition retrospectively controlled the movement's origin.
