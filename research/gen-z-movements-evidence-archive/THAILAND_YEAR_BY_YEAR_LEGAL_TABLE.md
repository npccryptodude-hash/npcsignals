# thailand — year-by-year legal table

version: **0.4**

scope: the legal and institutional aftermath of the 2020 youth-led democracy protest cycle.

this table is not a list of every prosecution. it records major milestones and aggregate indicators that help show how legal pressure developed over time.

| year | documented development | evidence status | significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 | youth-led democracy protests expand nationally; monarchy reform becomes an explicit public demand in parts of the movement | CONFIRMED | establishes the protest cycle examined in this archive |
| nov 2020 | prime minister prayut orders authorities to use "all laws and all articles" against democracy protesters; section 112 prosecutions resume after a hiatus | CONFIRMED | beginning of renewed lèse-majesté enforcement against movement-linked activists |
| 2021 | by HRW's 2022 world report, at least 151 people, including 12 children, had been charged under section 112 in relation to protest activity or social-media expression | CONFIRMED | shows rapid expansion of criminal cases after the protest peak |
| 2022 | emergency-decree covid restrictions are lifted in october, but protest-related cases continue | CONFIRMED | legal consequences outlast the emergency regime used during protests |
| 2023 | move forward wins the most seats in the general election but cannot form a government; section 112 reform remains institutionally blocked | CONFIRMED | electoral support linked to reform politics does not become executive control |
| 2024 | UN working group on arbitrary detention calls for arnon nampa's immediate release; activist netiporn sanesangkhom dies in pretrial detention | CONFIRMED | detention and bail practices become central human-rights issues |
| 2025 | HRW reports at least 1,959 people prosecuted for exercising expression / assembly rights, including at least 274 facing lèse-majesté charges; 1,469 people linked to 2020 protests still face emergency-decree prosecutions | CONFIRMED | demonstrates continuing legal tail five years after mobilization |
| sep 2025 | appeals court sentences democracy activist aekachai hongkangwan to more than 21 years for an offense linked to an october 2020 rally | CONFIRMED | severe sentence tied directly to protest-cycle conduct |
| 2026 | arnon nampa faces 14 royal-defamation cases; all bail applications reported by HRW have been denied | CONFIRMED | continued prosecution of a prominent 2020 movement figure |

## aggregate indicators

### 2021 benchmark
human rights watch reported at least **151 people**, including **12 children**, charged under section 112 after enforcement resumed against democracy protesters.

### 2025 benchmark
human rights watch reported:
- at least **1,959 people** prosecuted for expression and peaceful assembly;
- at least **274** facing lèse-majesté charges;
- at least **1,469 people** believed involved in 2020 protests still facing emergency-decree related prosecutions.

these categories overlap and must not be added together as if they were separate populations.

## interpretation

the evidence supports a **long-tail repression** model:

mass mobilization falls → prosecutions continue → detention and convictions accumulate → institutional reform remains blocked.

this does not establish that the movement had no long-term social or electoral influence.
