# bangladesh reform tracker

version: **0.5**

scope: reforms proposed after the 2024 uprising and their status under the elected 2026 government.

## transition

**CONFIRMED**

after the july–august 2024 uprising and sheikh hasina's departure, the interim government established reform commissions covering:

- constitution;
- electoral system;
- judiciary;
- public administration;
- policing;
- anti-corruption institutions.

## 2026 election and transfer of power

**CONFIRMED**

- general election held 12 february 2026;
- bnp won the majority;
- NCP won six of 300 seats according to Reuters reporting;
- tarique rahman sworn in as prime minister on 17 february 2026.

## reform implementation

**CONFIRMED / PARTIAL**

the elected government reviewed **133 ordinances** adopted under the interim government.

the breakdown:

- **98 retained unchanged**;
- **15 retained in revised form**;
- **20 repealed or allowed to lapse**

comes from **International Crisis Group**, as quoted in the UK government's october 2026 country assessment.

the UK government material also reports that:

- changes to criminal procedure addressed safeguards against arbitrary arrest;
- enforced-disappearance legislation passed in september 2026;
- national human rights commission legislation passed in september 2026.

## limitations and criticism

**SUPPORTED**

- some replacement laws were criticized by opposition and civil-society actors as weaker than interim-government versions;
- sources interviewed for the UK fact-finding material described progress on the broader reform agenda as **limited**, while noting that it was still early in the elected government's term.

## youth-movement outcome

**CONFIRMED**

the student-led movement had major mobilization and regime-change impact but did not dominate the february 2026 parliamentary result.

## analytical significance

bangladesh separates:

**regime removal** from **post-transition institutional control**.

some reforms survived the transition, but the youth movement did not itself become the dominant governing force.
