# bangladesh reform tracker

version: **0.2**

scope: reforms proposed after the 2024 uprising and their status under the elected 2026 government.

## transition

**CONFIRMED**

after the july–august 2024 uprising and sheikh hasina's departure, the interim government established reform commissions covering:

- constitution;
- electoral system;
- judiciary;
- public administration;
- policing;
- anti-corruption institutions.

the process later produced the **july national charter**, negotiated with political parties and other stakeholders.

## 2026 election and transfer of power

**CONFIRMED**

- general election held 12 february 2026;
- bnp won the majority;
- tarique rahman sworn in as prime minister on 17 february 2026;
- transition described by the uk home office summary as peaceful;
- eu election observers described the election as credible and genuinely competitive.

## reform implementation

**CONFIRMED / PARTIAL**

the bnp government reviewed ordinances adopted under the interim government.

the uk home office reports that:

- most were converted into law;
- changes to the code of criminal procedure included safeguards against arbitrary arrest;
- the enforced disappearance prevention and redress bill was passed on 7 september 2026;
- a national human rights commission bill was also passed on 7 september 2026.

## limitations and criticism

**SUPPORTED**

- some interim ordinances were allowed to lapse;
- some replacement laws were criticized by opposition and civil-society actors as weaker than the interim-government versions;
- fact-finding sources interviewed in june 2026 described progress on the july charter and wider reform recommendations as limited, while noting that the government was still early in its term.

## political-space change

**SUPPORTED**

the uk fact-finding mission reports broader political and civic space after august 2024, including greater room for political parties, journalists, civil society and rights groups to operate and speak publicly.

## youth-movement outcome

**CONFIRMED**

the student-led movement had major mobilization and regime-change impact but did not dominate the february 2026 parliamentary result.

## analytical significance

bangladesh separates:

**regime removal** from **post-transition institutional control**.

some reforms survived the transition, but the youth movement did not itself become the dominant governing force.
