## what is this study about?

this archive compares claims about burkina faso under ibrahim traoré with the evidence cited for them. it asks where a real policy or event becomes a stronger online story than its documentation supports.

it is not an assessment of whether a political leader is good or bad.

## what does the frozen study record?

the [23-row claim ledger](evidence-ledger.csv) separates policy and economic claims from claims about fabricated media, endorsements and hidden coordination. the [findings](findings.md) describe a documented policy core alongside stronger narratives that the study classifies as unsupported, contradicted or synthetic.

those are the frozen study's assessments. this reader guide does not independently re-adjudicate them or raise their evidence grades.

for example, an announcement of agricultural equipment supports the existence of a government programme. it does not by itself establish national food self-sufficiency. an industrial inauguration does not by itself establish the project's long-term output or economic effects.

## what cannot yet be reproduced from this package?

[sample-summary.md](sample-summary.md) reports 28 calibration items, 50 main-sample posts and 50 validation items. it also reports 32 narrative clusters within the main sample. the 23-row claim ledger is a different unit of analysis; it is not an item-by-item record of those 128 sample observations.

individual post lists, their coding and the mapping into clusters were not located in this study's deposited files. the ledger itself has no direct source urls. source links survive in the [archived article draft's references](article/medium-draft.md), but they do not provide a complete claim-to-source or sample-item mapping.

these gaps limit independent reproduction of the sampling and network conclusions. they do not prove that a claim is false. even a fully preserved convenience sample would not measure the prevalence of a narrative across the internet. attribution of central coordination and the long-term effects of reforms remain unresolved.
