# Methodology

## Unit of analysis

One discrete empirical claim or claim family from the 24 September 2026 UN General Assembly speech.

## Scoring rules

| Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|
| PASS / MOSTLY SUPPORTED | Strong evidence supports the core claim |
| MIXED / MISLEADING | A factual core exists, but wording materially overstates, compresses or misattributes the evidence |
| UNSUPPORTED | Evidence is insufficient for the asserted claim |
| CONTRADICTED | Strong available evidence conflicts with the claim |
| HOLD / EXCLUDED | No stable factual benchmark can be applied yet |

## Special treatment

### Counterfactual claims
Statements such as "without this action, we would all be dead" are not forced into the factual denominator.

### Legal claims
Genocide, occupation and other legal characterisations are treated through relevant legal institutions and established legal frameworks, not as ordinary fact-check rows.

### Motive claims
Claims about why another actor funded, acted or spoke are not treated as established unless direct evidence supports the motive.

### Broad military claims
Claims such as "crushed an army" must be decomposed into measurable components before scoring.

### Government self-reporting
Israeli government or military records can establish what Israel says it did. They do not alone establish the wider effect, legality or independent accuracy of the action.

## Freeze rule

This first-pass ledger is frozen on 25 September 2026. Later evidence must be added through annotations or versioned revisions rather than silently changing the original record.
