## Research question

Does demographic change alter electoral competition in a structurally observable way, and if so, through which mechanisms?

DDR-01 separates two channels:

1. **Direct channel** — changes in the composition of the participating electorate.
2. **Indirect channel** — changes in the behaviour, priorities or party competition of the wider electorate.

The test does **not** assume that demographic change produces a fixed left/right outcome.

## Evaluation framework

The direct channel is evaluated as a chain:

**population → eligibility → participation → preference → geographic distribution → institutional conversion**

The indirect channel is evaluated through evidence that demographic change or immigration-related issues alter:

- issue salience
- party positioning
- coalition structure
- voter priorities
- electoral competition among voters outside the changing demographic group

## What Germany adds to DDR-01

Germany does not provide a clean causal test of demographic change.

It provides something else: a useful falsification environment for simplistic directional claims.

Within the same national system and the same month:

- AfD finished first in Saxony-Anhalt.
- AfD finished first in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern.
- The Left Party finished first in Berlin.

Those outcomes occurred under different regional economic, demographic, historical and issue-salience conditions.

DDR-01 therefore does not interpret the German sequence as evidence that demographic change mechanically produces movement toward any one political side.

Instead, Germany strengthens the need to keep separate:

**demographic composition**

from

**issue salience**

from

**government dissatisfaction**

from

**regional political history**

from

**electoral outcome**

The German observation is compatible with the indirect-channel mechanism, because immigration and demographic questions form part of political competition.

It is not sufficient to estimate the causal magnitude of that mechanism.

## Falsification checks

### H1 — Fixed left/right effect

**Result: FALSIFIED**

The evidence does not support a universal rule that demographic change produces movement toward one political side.

Germany's September 2026 state-election sequence is consistent with this conclusion: markedly different regional political outcomes occurred within the same country and month.

### H2 — Demography is only background context

**Result: REJECTED**

In multiple cases, demographic composition enters electoral competition through observable participation, preference and geographic mechanisms.

### H3 — Composition alone explains electoral effects

**Result: INSUFFICIENT**

Raw population share is not enough. Eligibility, turnout, preference, location, issue salience and electoral institutions condition political effects.

### H4 — A universal large causal effect can be estimated

**Result: UNRESOLVED**

DDR-01 supports the existence of mechanisms but does not establish a single cross-country causal magnitude.
