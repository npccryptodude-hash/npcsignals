## Research question

Does demographic change alter electoral competition in a structurally observable way, and if so, through which mechanisms?

DDR-01 separates two channels:

1. **Direct channel** — changes in the composition of the participating electorate.
2. **Indirect channel** — changes in the behaviour, priorities or party competition of the wider electorate.

The test does **not** assume that demographic change produces a fixed left/right outcome.

## Evaluation framework

The direct channel is evaluated as a chain:

**population → eligibility → participation → preference → geographic distribution → institutional conversion**

A demographic shift can be politically relevant without every link being equally strong.

The indirect channel is evaluated through evidence that demographic change or immigration-related issues alter:

- issue salience
- party positioning
- coalition structure
- voter priorities
- electoral competition among voters outside the changing demographic group

## Falsification checks

### H1 — Fixed left/right effect

**Result: FALSIFIED**

The evidence does not support a universal rule that demographic change produces movement toward one political side. Behaviour differs across countries, origin groups, generations, electoral systems and time periods.

### H2 — Demography is only background context

**Result: REJECTED**

In multiple cases, demographic composition enters electoral competition through observable participation, preference and geographic mechanisms.

### H3 — Composition alone explains electoral effects

**Result: INSUFFICIENT**

Raw population share is not enough. Eligibility, turnout, preference, location and electoral institutions condition the effect.

### H4 — A universal large causal effect can be estimated

**Result: UNRESOLVED**

DDR-01 supports the existence of mechanisms but does not establish a single cross-country causal magnitude.
