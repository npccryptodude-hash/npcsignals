# Norway Welfare Infrastructure 2001–2036

**Status:** Research package  
**Freeze date:** 2026-09-30  
**Research window:** 2001–2025  
**Programme window:** 2027–2036  
**Preservation window:** 2037–2050  

## Research question

Can Norway finance and execute a ten-year restoration of education, hospital and elderly-care infrastructure — including necessary equipment and working conditions — without undermining long-term fiscal sustainability?

## Core finding

The available evidence does not support **aggregate national financial scarcity** as a sufficient primary explanation.

Local and sector-specific scarcity can still be real because Norway combines high national financial capacity with fragmented asset responsibility, uneven lifecycle maintenance, competing operating demands and increasing demographic pressure.

> **The problem appears less about whether Norway has money, and more about whether the system preserves public capital efficiently over time.**

## Programme model

The research tests a maximum public-investment envelope of **NOK 250 billion for 2027–2036**.

This is **not** a demonstrated spending requirement.

> **NOK 250 billion is a ceiling, not a spending target.**

Programme logic:

> **Audit → Prioritise → Restore → Adapt Capacity → Maintain → Measure**

## Evidence labels

**CONFIRMED** — directly supported by a reliable source.  
**SUPPORTED** — multiple pieces of evidence point in the same direction.  
**DERIVED** — calculated from sourced data.  
**MODELLED** — scenario or planning assumption.  
**UNRESOLVED** — insufficient evidence to close the question.  
**CONTRADICTED / NOT ESTABLISHED** — evidence conflicts with the claim or causality has not been shown.
