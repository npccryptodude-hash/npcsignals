# Methodology

## Scope

This project examines physical welfare infrastructure in three sectors:

- schools
- hospitals
- elderly care

It does not treat foreign aid as a competing funding pot and does not attempt to attribute responsibility to individual political parties.

## Time structure

### 2001–2025 — Historical evidence
How did national financial capacity, public spending, maintenance and physical welfare infrastructure develop?

### 2027–2036 — Programme period
What would a realistic ten-year restoration and capacity programme require?

### 2037–2050 — Preservation period
Can the restored assets be maintained without recreating the backlog?

## Four-ledger rule

The analysis keeps four ledgers separate:

1. **Existing Asset Restoration**
2. **Already Funded Baseline**
3. **Future Capacity**
4. **Lifecycle Maintenance**

This prevents gross need from being confused with new funding need.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Primary documents and raw data
2. Official records and direct statements
3. Peer-reviewed or methodologically transparent research
4. High-quality secondary reporting
5. Specialist analysis
6. Social-media claims and commentary

## Analytical safeguards

- investment is not the same as maintenance
- gross need is not the same as incremental need
- correlation is not causation
- demographic expansion is not maintenance backlog
- existing funded projects must be matched before subtraction
- unresolved questions remain unresolved
- model assumptions must be labelled as model assumptions

## Update discipline

No figure should silently replace an earlier figure.

Future updates should record:

> previous estimate → new estimate → reason for revision
