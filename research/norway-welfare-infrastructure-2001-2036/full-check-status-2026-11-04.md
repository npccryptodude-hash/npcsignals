# Full check status — 2026-11-04

**Project:** Norway Welfare Infrastructure 2001–2036  
**Status:** Research only — not published outside GitHub  
**Purpose:** Consolidate the post-NOU audit and identify what is closed, what is supported, and what still blocks a defensible programme-wide incremental estimate.

## Executive result

The original research thesis survives the full check, but the NOK 250bn figure has been materially narrowed in meaning.

It is no longer treated as a plausible estimate of total need.

It is now:

> **an upper model scenario for incremental programme capacity over ten years, conditional on baseline subtraction, project overlap, labour capacity, organisational capacity and alternative service models.**

The full check strengthens the argument that Norway's problem is not reducible to aggregate national financial scarcity.

It also strengthens the opposite caution:

> **financial capacity does not prove execution capacity.**

## 1. Financial capacity

**Status: SUPPORTED**

Norway has substantial national fiscal capacity, but municipal and sector-specific constraints remain real.

The macro comparison still supports using NOK 250bn as a stress-test scale, not as a spending recommendation.

**No change to core classification.**

## 3. School infrastructure

The technical need remains material, but the national 2021–2025 investment baseline for KOSTRA function 222 has not yet been extracted from the authoritative national Statbank series in a reproducible form.

Known:
- correct KOSTRA function: **222 School premises**
- correct property-management source hierarchy: table 12559 first
- national aggregates are affected by documented corrections and accounting effects
- 2024 contains a classification effect that makes single-year interpretation weaker

**Status:** BASELINE UNRESOLVED.

No programme-wide net estimate can be frozen until this is closed or explicitly bounded.

## 4. Elderly-care infrastructure and capacity

The full check now separates three concepts:

1. existing asset renewal;
2. net new round-the-clock capacity;
3. alternative/home-based capacity.

Husbanken commitments 2021–2025:

> **5,002 grant-supported places**  
> average **~1,000 per year**

These include both renewal and growth.

SSB institution capacity:

- 2021: **39,305**
- 2025: **39,915**

Government documents explicitly state that total round-the-clock capacity has increased only slowly because municipalities also renew and close old or unsuitable places.

Updated SSB projection:

> **~22,800 additional round-the-clock places from 2024 to 2035**

Mechanical average:

> **~2,073 net additional places per year**

2027 proposal:

> **~1,750 grant-supported places**, with 30% of the commitment frame reserved for net growth.

**Status: STRUCTURAL CAPACITY GAP — SUPPORTED.**

The historical construction/grant pace is not sufficient evidence of the future net capacity required.

The 97–111bn care-capacity calculation remains a gross physical scale test, not an incremental budget need.

## 5. Labour capacity

NOU 2026:12 materially strengthens this part of the research.

Projected health-trained labour gap around 2040:

> **~42,000 FTEs**

**Status: CORE CONSTRAINT — STRONGLY SUPPORTED.**

No major capital project should be prioritised without an explicit staffing and operating model.

A building that cannot be staffed is not a capacity solution.

## 6. Organisational capacity

NOU 2026:12 identifies weak capacity, competence and professional robustness in many small municipalities.

**Status: INDEPENDENT CONSTRAINT — SUPPORTED.**

Capital alone may not solve delivery problems where planning, procurement, staffing or service structure is insufficient.

## 7. Baseline rule

The full check locks the following equation:

> **gross technical/demographic need  
> − already-planned relevant investment  
> − documented replacement/rehabilitation overlap  
> − baseline net new capacity  
> − alternative capacity solutions  
> = possible incremental programme need**

No term may be inserted as an assumed deduction without source-level support.

## 8. Four mandatory programme gates

Every major project must pass:

### Gate A — Baseline
Would this investment happen anyway?

### Gate B — Labour
Can the capacity be staffed sustainably?

### Gate C — Organisation
Can the owner plan, procure and operate it effectively?

### Gate D — Alternative solution
Can the same service need be met more effectively through rehabilitation, technology, home-based care, shared capacity or another service model?

## 10. Can a programme-wide incremental range be frozen now?

**No.**

Two material blockers remain:

1. authoritative national 2021–2025 investment baseline for KOSTRA function 222;
2. authoritative national 2021–2025 investment baseline for KOSTRA function 261.

Hospital overlap is partly bounded but not fully quantified.

Care-capacity baseline is structurally understood, but future baseline net-growth volume remains uncertain.

Publishing a low/mid/high programme-wide net number now would create false precision.

## 11. What is sufficiently strong for a future article

The following claims are currently strong enough to use, with caveats:

- aggregate national financial scarcity is not a sufficient primary explanation;
- investment and technical backlog can coexist;
- restoration and future capacity are different problems;
- health and care labour supply is a hard constraint;
- organisational capacity matters independently of money;
- historical care-capacity growth is too weak to assume that future demographic demand will be met automatically;
- existing planned hospital investment must be subtracted by scope before any incremental hospital requirement is claimed;
- NOK 250bn is a stress-test ceiling, not a documented need.

## 12. What must not be claimed yet

Do not state that:

- Norway needs NOK 250bn;
- NOK 269–313bn is the net required programme;
- the entire RIF hospital estimate remains unfunded;
- the RHF investment plan eliminates the hospital backlog;
- 22,800 new traditional nursing-home places must be built;
- historical grant commitments equal net capacity growth;
- school-building investment baseline has been quantified nationally;
- deferred maintenance is the sole or dominant cause of all backlog.

## 15. Continued evidence hardening — 2026-11-04

### Haugesund hospital

Helse Fonna's own development plan explicitly states that parts of the estate require upgrading and refurbishment because of **maintenance backlog**, that technical systems must be replaced or upgraded in several buildings, and that parts of the required physical improvement at Haugesund hospital are being carried out through building stages 1 and 2.

This strengthens the classification of Haugesund BT2 from a merely mixed capital project to:

> **MIXED PROJECT WITH DIRECTLY DOCUMENTED BACKLOG-RELEVANT COMPONENT**

No kroner deduction is frozen because the available project documentation does not separate maintenance/backlog remediation from new capacity and functional redesign.

### KOSTRA 222 / 261

SSB's PxWebApi v2 documentation confirms that authoritative table extracts can be generated by GET query and that table 12559 is the correct property-management source. The required national series remain conceptually available, but the current research environment has not yet produced a reproducible national EAK extract for both 222 and 261.

The evidence boundary therefore remains unchanged:

> **do not replace missing national Statbank values with municipality-level proxies or secondary reconstructions.**

### Implication

The audit can continue to harden project-level overlap without freezing the programme-wide net range prematurely.


## 16. Provisional pre-KOSTRA incremental range

Because the authoritative national KOSTRA 222 and 261 series remain unresolved, the programme-wide range is now calculated in a deliberately conservative form that assigns **zero deduction** to school- and institution-building baseline investment.

This means the result is more likely to overstate than understate incremental need.

### Starting gross range

> **NOK 269–313bn**

### Hospital overlap scenarios

- low deduction: **0.611bn** — Tier 1 rehabilitation candidate only
- midpoint deduction: **2.0bn** — modelled partial allocation across Tier 1 + Tier 2
- high deduction: **4.333bn** — full identified Tier 1 + Tier 2 project-frame ceiling; not treated as a factual overlap

### Care-capacity baseline scenarios

The 2027 state budget proposes support for about **1,750 round-the-clock care places**, with **30% of the commitment frame reserved for net growth**.

For a transparent ten-year baseline sensitivity, three modelled annual net-growth paths are tested:

- low baseline: **525 net places/year** — equivalent to 30% of 1,750
- midpoint baseline: **875 net places/year** — 50% of 1,750
- high baseline: **1,225 net places/year** — 70% of 1,750

Using the 2027 grant frame and proposed 55% grant rate implies a rough project-cost scale of approximately **NOK 4.22m per place**.

Over ten years this produces modelled baseline capacity values of approximately:

- 525/year: **NOK 22.2bn**
- 875/year: **NOK 36.9bn**
- 1,225/year: **NOK 51.7bn**

These are **MODELLED BASELINE SENSITIVITIES**, not forecasts.

### Result

#### Lower incremental scenario
269 − 4.333 − 51.7 ≈

> **NOK 213bn**

#### Midpoint scenario
291 − 2.0 − 36.9 ≈

> **NOK 252bn**

#### Upper incremental scenario
313 − 0.611 − 22.2 ≈

> **NOK 290bn**

### Interpretation

> **Provisional pre-KOSTRA incremental range: approximately NOK 213–290bn, midpoint ~NOK 252bn.**

This range is **not final**.

It intentionally applies:
- no school-building baseline deduction;
- no institution-building baseline deduction;
- no quantified alternative-service-model saving;
- only a limited hospital-overlap sensitivity.

Therefore the final defensible incremental range may move lower once KOSTRA 222/261 and alternative-capacity effects are quantified.

### Consequence for the NOK 250bn ceiling

The midpoint landing close to NOK 250bn does **not** validate the original 250bn figure.

It only shows that, under one explicit set of conservative assumptions, the earlier ceiling remains within the centre of the current sensitivity range.

The research must therefore describe NOK 250bn as:

> **a retained model ceiling that remains plausible under current sensitivities, not an empirically demonstrated requirement.**
