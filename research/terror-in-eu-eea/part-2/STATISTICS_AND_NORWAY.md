## What PST assesses

Europol's annual report describes events that Member States submitted for a reporting period. Norway's Police Security Service (PST) assesses threats facing Norway prospectively. Its 2026 National Threat Assessment discusses extreme Islamist and right-wing extremist threats in the politically motivated violence section. The assessment concerns possible future violence and evaluates the actors and conditions relevant to Norway. A ratio of past EU entries provides no direct estimate of that threat. The assessments can therefore coexist with a higher left-wing/anarchist count than right-wing count in particular EU reporting years. [PST, National Threat Assessment 2026](https://www.pst.no/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/National-Threat-Assessment-2026.pdf)

Norwegian historical evidence exists at case level. The official Extremism Commission discusses the 2019 Bærum mosque attack and killing and the 25 June 2022 attack in Oslo. Those are historical examples in Norway, separate from the Europol denominators. The commission's examples, annual threat assessments and the existing [country-year coverage grid](../data/country_year_coverage_2015_2025.csv) do not establish a harmonised Norwegian calendar-year series for 2015–2025 with comparable completed, failed and foiled coverage. Missing observations should remain missing. [NOU 2024:3, introductory discussion](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/dokumenter/nou-2024-3/id3027182/?ch=3)
