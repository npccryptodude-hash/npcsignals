# Italy's concentration in left-wing and anarchist terrorism reporting, 2022–2025

## What the reported entries show

Europol's original annual reporting cohorts place a substantial share of the EU's left-wing and anarchist attack entries in Italy. The following figures reproduce the validated Part 1 category and country subtotals; they are **reported statistical entries**, not a census of physical attacks, persons or organisations.

| Reference year | Italy | EU category total | Italy share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2022 | 12 | 18 | 66.7% |
| 2023 | 30 | 32 | 93.8% |
| 2024 | 18 | 21 | 85.7% |
| 2025 | 11 | 12 | 91.7% |
| **Four original cohorts** | **71** | **83** | **85.5%** |

The four-year sum is an arithmetic description of the cited original reporting cohorts, not a harmonised event-year series. No inference about the underlying incidence of political violence, the number of organisations, or relative risk in Italy versus other countries follows from the percentages alone. The 2023 country/outcome descriptions retain the Spanish allocation conflict recorded in Part 1; the category totals above are unchanged.

## What Europol describes, beyond the counts

**2023.** Europol's TE-SAT 2024 reports 32 left-wing/anarchist attacks, 30 in Italy; 23 completed, eight failed and one foiled. Its narrative says most attacks targeted property and infrastructure and were associated with anti-state and anti-capitalist discourse. It also identifies solidarity with an imprisoned FAI/FRI-linked anarchist as an important theme. These are Europol's characterisations of reported activity, not proof that a single organisation directed the 30 Italian entries.

**2024.** TE-SAT 2025 reports 21 entries, 18 in Italy; 17 completed and four failed. It describes propaganda relating to anti-capitalism, anti-militarism, imprisonment, international conflicts and other causes. It also describes a case classified in the anarchist terrorism context where many participants reportedly lacked ideological affiliation and were motivated primarily by financial gain. This is a warning against inferring each participant's motive from the statistical category.

**2025.** TE-SAT 2026 reports 12 entries, 11 in Italy and one in Greece; 11 completed and one failed. It describes attacks mainly against property and reports no casualties in this category. These aggregate facts do not identify perpetrators, establish an organisation-wide objective or measure the success of any attempt at institutional disruption.

## What can and cannot be concluded

- **Supported:** Italy accounts for 71 of 83 left-wing/anarchist entries across the four original EU cohorts (2022–2025). The concentration is large and persistent in these reported data.
- **Reported institutional analysis:** Europol describes anti-state, anti-capitalist and other motivations in selected years and cases. Those narratives should be attributed to Europol and tested against event-specific primary sources.
- **Not established:** That all 71 Italian entries were carried out by FAI/FRI, one coordinated network or even by identifiable members of stable organisations.
- **Not established:** That the 71 entries represent 71 unique physical events with uniformly comparable national terrorism classifications.
- **Not established:** That disruption of institutions was achieved, or that it is the common objective of all anarchist or left-wing actors.
- **Not established:** That this EU distribution predicts the future terrorism threat in Norway.

## Questions for event-level research

1. Which Italian entries have a verifiable national police, prosecutor or court record identifying the act and legal classification?
2. Which entries have only a claimed responsibility label, and which have judicially established perpetrators or organisational links?
3. Do target selection and claims differ between anti-prison, anti-militarist, anti-capitalist and other stated causes?
4. How do Italian legal/reporting practices compare with those of other EU states, without presuming the cause of the concentration?
5. Can the 2022–2025 entries be linked to identifiable actors without double counting or substituting ideological similarity for operational proof?
