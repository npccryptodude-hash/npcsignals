# left-wing and anarchist terrorism in European reporting, 2015–2025

## Ideas, movements and violent actors

Anarchism is a family of political philosophies that questions the legitimacy of coercive hierarchy and state authority. Some traditions explicitly favour nonviolence. Legal definitions and statistical categories concern the acts and aims recorded by authorities. The combined Europol heading includes distinct revolutionary left-wing and anarchist traditions, so it cannot supply an anarchist-only count. ## Does violence serve institutional disruption?

For Revolutionary Class Self-Defense, the US NCTC explicitly assesses an anti-capitalist and anti-authoritarian objective and describes violence as intended to disrupt Greek political and economic institutions. Its attributed examples concern government and transport institutions. The classification of this objective is **reported official assessment**. The occurrence of specific acts, their stated rationale and their long-term effects are separate propositions. There is no measure here of actual institutional weakening.

The FAI/FRI-linked Rome claim supports a narrower observation about claimed action, with no verified command link. These cases give actor-specific support to the disruption hypothesis. The evidence cannot establish that disruption explains every entry, or that it is a common objective of anarchism. Target categories in the cases include public institutions and transport-related property. Their relative prominence across the full period cannot be estimated from the identified cases. Selection reflects the availability of records, and the archive contains unresolved entries. [Sources and locators](SOURCES.md#left-wing-and-anarchist-cases)

## What the counts say

The original category totals for 2022–2025 are 18, 32, 21 and 12. Italy accounts for 12, 30, 18 and 11 of those entries respectively. The 2023 edition's country and outcome descriptions contain a disputed Spanish allocation, recorded in the existing [2023 register](../2023/BASELINE.md). The validated category subtotals do not depend on resolving it.

For 2025, Europol reports 11 completed and one failed in this category, and describes the targets mainly as property. It reports no casualties for this category in its summary. That is an edition-specific observation. The same edition records five right-wing entries, one completed with one fatality, and 24 jihadist entries, including nine completed with five fatalities and 81 injuries. Twelve divided by five is **2.4**, a ratio of reported entries. Completed entries alone have a different distribution, and fatalities are a separate measure. The case material cannot establish a category-wide share of organisationally directed versus individually inspired incidents.
