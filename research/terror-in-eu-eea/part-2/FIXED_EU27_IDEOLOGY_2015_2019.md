# EU28 versus fixed EU27: ideological composition, 2015–2019

## Why the UK subtraction matters

The first five original Europol cohorts in this investigation use EU28. For comparing them with later EU27 editions, Part 1 reconstructed fixed-EU27 category subtotals from the original UK country rows. This subtraction is arithmetic; it does not harmonise national classifications, reporting thresholds or occurrence years.

| Year | Original EU28 total | UK entries | Derived fixed EU27 | Jihadist EU27 | Right-wing EU27 | Left-wing/anarchist EU27 | Separatist EU27 | Other/residual EU27 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2015 | 211 | 103 | 108 | 17 | 9 | 13 | 65 | 4 |
| 2016 | 142 | 76 | 66 | 13 | 1 | 27 | 23 | 2 |
| 2017 | 205 | 107 | 98 | 19 | 0 | 24 | 49 | 6 |
| 2018 | 129 | 60 | 69 | 20 | 1 | 19 | 27 | 2 |
| 2019 | 119 | 64 | 55 | 18 | 2 | 26 | 1 | 8 |

**Important exception:** In 2015, the UK submitted 103 entries in an undifferentiated/unspecified column. Subtracting these produces an EU27 remainder for the *reported table*, but does not establish that the UK had no jihadist, right-wing or other ideological incidents. In 2017, the UK contribution includes 14 jihadist and five right-wing entries; those cannot be treated as EU27 incidents. The UK category allocation changes across editions.

## Distinct observations

- The fixed-EU27 jihadist category totals for these five reporting years are 17, 13, 19, 20 and 18. This is a narrow observation about the published statistical categories, not a claim that risk, fatalities, ideological influence or actual event incidence stayed constant.
- The fixed-EU27 left-wing/anarchist category totals are 13, 27, 24, 19 and 26. These are a combined category; they do not identify anarchist-only events or any particular organisation.
- The separatist EU27 remainder moves from 65 in 2015 to one in 2019. That large variation must not be attributed to an organisational collapse without event-level, national and reporting-practice evidence.
- The 2015 original publication contains an unresolved figure/text conflict (193 versus 211). The table uses the annex-reconciled 211, as Part 1 does.
- The 2018 Dutch annex contains a printed cell inconsistency documented in Part 1; the table uses its reconciled 129 total.

## Implications for organisational research

No ideological-category subtotal can identify a perpetrator. An organisation profile needs a case-level join: occurrence, national legal status, attribution source, claimed or established responsibility, and evidence for organisational membership or direction. This is particularly important where an edition groups UK security incidents, unspecified acts, or retrospectively reported events differently from other editions.
