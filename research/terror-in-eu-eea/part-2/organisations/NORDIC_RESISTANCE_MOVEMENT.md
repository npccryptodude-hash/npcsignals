# Nordic Resistance Movement (NRM)

## Identity and documented history

The US Department of State's [14 June 2024 designation fact sheet](https://2021-2025.state.gov/terrorist-designations-of-nordic-resistance-movement-and-three-leaders/) says the organisation was formed in **1997** as the Swedish Resistance Movement and adopted the Nordic Resistance Movement name in **2016**. The US account describes its base in Sweden and branches in Norway, Denmark, Iceland and Finland, and reports that Finland banned the organisation in **2020**. These are US government statements. The Finnish Supreme Court's official summary of KKO:2020:68 was subsequently reviewed below; the full Finnish-language judgment and national membership records have not been independently reviewed.

## Stated political objectives and ideology

The same fact sheet attributes to NRM an objective of replacing Nordic democracies with a united **ethnically defined Nordic state**. It describes the group's platform as neo-Nazi, racist, antisemitic, anti-immigrant and anti-LGBTQI+. This is a US official account that explicitly attributes the stated political objective to the organisation; the original NRM programme has not been independently authenticated here.

The existence of a political programme does not establish that every supporter, person with similar views or defendant in a right-wing case is an NRM member.

## Reported violence and preparations

The US State Department states that NRM members and leaders carried out violence against political opponents, demonstrators, journalists and other perceived adversaries. It also reports weapons and explosives preparation and training in violent tactics. The fact sheet does not enumerate judicial case identifiers, defendant-specific convictions or a count of attacks matching Europol's annual statistical cells. These are therefore **US executive-branch assessments**, not independently reconstructed European criminal judgments.

The existing [2021 Swedish case relationship record](../../2021/proceedings_relationships.csv), SWE-R1, preserves a separate **Europol-reported association** with NRM. That case-level association does not itself establish membership, assistance or organisational direction.

## Designation and named leadership, 2024

On **14 June 2024**, the US designated NRM and three leaders as **Specially Designated Global Terrorists (SDGT)** under Executive Order 13224. The fact sheet identifies Tor Fredrik Vejdeland as leader and national-council head, Pär Öberg as a council member and head of the parliamentary branch, and Leif Robert Eklund as a council member and Swedish divisional coordinator. These are leadership identifications **as stated in the 2024 US notice**, not verified current officeholders.

The designation blocks covered property subject to US jurisdiction and generally prohibits US persons from transacting with the designated parties. **SDGT is not the same legal instrument as a US Foreign Terrorist Organization (FTO) designation, an EU listing, a Finnish prohibition or a criminal conviction.** Do not collapse those statuses into one.

## Finnish Supreme Court: original judicial determination (verified 20 November 2026)

The Finnish Supreme Court's own English-language summary of **KKO:2020:68**, issued **22 September 2020**, is now directly verified: [official court summary](https://www.korkeinoikeus.fi/en/news/news-archive/kko202068-disbandment-of-an-association/). The court affirmed disbandment under **section 43(1)(1) of the Finnish Associations Act**. This is an association-law ruling, **not a criminal terrorism conviction or a judicial finding that NRM directed the 2021 Swedish case**.

The court identified as established the association's national-socialist objectives, the pursuit of a change from representative democracy to the association's rule, and severe restrictions on others' fundamental rights. It examined the association's website and described racist material and Holocaust denial. The court expressly noted that the association's formal rules restricted violence to self-defence, **but** found that leaders or active members had committed assaults and other offences, that violent offences had resulted in convictions, and that the organisation had tolerated or positively treated such conduct. It found violence at least tacitly accepted in the association's activities and concluded that unlawful activity was sufficiently essential to require disbandment rather than a caution.

**Evidence boundary:** This directly supports the Finnish judicial findings about the association's objectives and activities. It does not establish the guilt of every member, a Europe-wide NRM attack count, or command in a separate incident. The Supreme Court summary is an official court publication; the full Finnish-language judgment may provide additional reasoning.
