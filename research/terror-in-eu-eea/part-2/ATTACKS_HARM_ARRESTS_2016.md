# The 2016 contrast: attack frequency, victims and arrests

## Three different denominators

Europol's TE-SAT 2017 reports 142 terrorist attack entries across the original EU28 in 2016. The ideological distribution was: 99 separatist, 27 left-wing/anarchist, 13 jihadist, one right-wing and two not specified. These counts are **reported attacks**, not a measure of total violence, the number of offenders or the severity of harm.

The same report records **142 deaths and 379 injuries** in its headline figures. Its Annex 1 attributes **135 of the 142 deaths and 374 of the 379 injuries** to religiously inspired terrorism, even though that category accounts for only 13 of the 142 attack entries. The report's specific incident injury numbers are internally incompatible with these aggregates: Brussels (340), Nice (201) and Berlin (56) sum to 597, already exceeding the headline 379. Preserve the inconsistency; do not silently reconcile it or infer a consistent case-level injury series.

The report's Annex 2 records **1,002 terrorism-related arrests**: 718 religiously inspired, 84 separatist, 31 left-wing/anarchist, 12 right-wing and 157 not specified. Arrests describe a different population and cannot be divided by the attack count to derive a perpetrator rate.

| Measure, original 2016 edition | Most numerous category | Observed count | Key limitation |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Reported attacks | Separatist | 99 of 142 | UK reported 76 Northern Ireland-related entries; country and category practices differ. |
| Fatalities | Religiously inspired / jihadist chapter | 135 of 142 | Event and victim coverage must follow the edition's definitions. |
| Injuries (published headline) | Religiously inspired / jihadist chapter | 374 of 379 | Incident examples contradict the aggregate; see the 2016 baseline. |
| Terrorism-related arrests | Religiously inspired | 718 of 1,002 | Persons arrested, not attacks or convictions. |

## Why this matters to Part 2

A claim such as 'the largest category is the greatest threat' cannot be derived from an attack count alone. The 2016 edition provides a concrete counterexample: separatist entries dominate the attack total, while the religiously inspired category dominates recorded deaths and injuries. Neither historical measure by itself is a prospective Norwegian threat assessment.

The 2016 UK category also requires care: Europol reports all UK attacks as Northern Ireland-related and does not provide the same breakdown for UK arrests. Comparisons with the EU27 series after 2019 require a fixed geographical scope.
