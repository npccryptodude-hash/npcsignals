# ideology index

| Europol category | How to read the label | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Jihadist | A violent extremist classification invoking a particular interpretation of Islam to justify violence. Organisational projects and personal motives differ. | Religious belief, practice and community affiliation establish no terrorist connection. |
| Right-wing | Terrorism attributed to extreme-right motives. Some examples involve racial hierarchy, antisemitism or hostility to groups perceived as outsiders. | A lawful political position, even a radical one, supplies no evidence of an offence. |
| Left-wing and anarchist | A combined reporting category that can include revolutionary left-wing and anti-authoritarian violent actors. Some reject state authority, while other revolutionary traditions seek to reorganise or control it. | Treat the aggregate as combined unless the underlying source supports a narrower classification. Anarchist philosophy includes nonviolent traditions. |
| Ethno-nationalist and separatist | A category often associated with claims to independence, sovereignty or territorial autonomy. | Advocacy of self-determination is distinct from evidence of violent conduct and terrorist intent. |
| Other, mixed or insufficiently classified | Residual labels depend on the edition. Other, single-issue and unspecified can carry different meanings. Mixed motives require their own evidence. | A missing category is an unknown, and a residual entry may carry no identifiable ideology. |

Europol's category descriptions, national reporting and legal outcomes have distinct scopes. Article 3 of Directive (EU) 2017/541 describes a legal test involving specified intentional acts, potential serious harm and particular aims. The ideological label alone does not establish those elements. [Europol category methodology and legal source](SOURCES.md#category-definitions)

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning in this research |
|---|---|
| Ideology | A set of ideas about society and power. |
| Movement | A broader field of people, groups or initiatives sharing themes. It may contain lawful activity and is not necessarily coordinated. |
| Organisation | A named entity for which evidence supports a collective identity. A name used to claim an act may be a claimed label without a verified membership or stable structure. |
| Operational network | Documented people or entities linked by activities such as assistance, communication or direction in a specific case. Each connection needs its own evidence. |
| Individual attacker | A person established or attributed to a specific act. Inspiration, membership and command are separate propositions. |
| Stated objective | A goal expressed by an actor or reported by a source. A stated justification does not independently establish motive, identity or responsibility. |
| Statistical entry | A unit in an edition's reported total, including completed, failed or foiled entries. One entry can represent more than one physical act. |
| Confirmed | A primary record supports the precise proposition. A police notice can confirm an occurrence without confirming a perpetrator. |
| Reported | Attributed to reporting, an official assessment or a claim. Specify the source role, including when an official publication repeats a claim. |
| Disputed | Source observations or substantive accounts conflict. |
| Unresolved | Available evidence does not support a conclusion on that proposition. |
