# Separatist and ethno-nationalist reporting: statistical cohorts versus organisations

The Europol category combines diverse conflicts and legal classifications. This chapter separates the published attack entries from the political aims, claims and organisational responsibility that would require event-level evidence. Peaceful advocacy of independence or self-determination is not terrorism.

These totals must not be summed into an event-year series without resolving retrospective allocation and national scope. In particular, the 70 Corsican entries are a 2023 **reporting cohort**, not 70 events that all occurred in 2023. The 2023 French country total of 80 includes other categories; the 70 Corsican entries are its separatist subtotal.

## What is known about organisation and responsibility

The annual 2023 register establishes the retrospective occurrence-year split, but the 70 individual Corsican events are **not** independently allocated to named perpetrators or organisations in the current incident crosswalk. A regional conflict context is not proof that a particular movement or group committed each event. A responsibility claim, where present, would need its own source and event match.

The Northern Ireland entries from the original EU28 cohorts also reflect a source-defined mixture of terrorism and security-related incidents. They cannot automatically be treated as a single named organisation's operations, nor can a Northern Ireland shooting or bombing count from PSNI be added to an EU attack cohort without checking units and dates.

The 2020 EU27 separatist count of 14 remains an edition-specific statistic; the current annual baseline does not establish a complete case-level organisational distribution or a verified outcome split.

## What must be established before organisational histories

1. **Corsica:** obtain event-level French police, prosecutor or judicial material and determine which of the 70 reported entries can be linked to a named actor or responsibility claim.
2. **Northern Ireland:** distinguish the Europol historical UK security-event units from PSNI calendar-year or financial-year shootings, bombings and terrorism measures.
3. **Organisation:** identify each group's own documented political objective, period of activity, and whether its name is an established entity or a claim label.
4. **Attribution:** distinguish group statement, police assessment, indictment and final judicial finding. Do not infer organisational command from a common territorial cause.
5. **Time:** keep occurrence date, reporting year and later judicial outcome in separate fields.
6. **Comparability:** retain original EU28 and EU27 editions and separately label fixed-EU27 arithmetic where supported.
