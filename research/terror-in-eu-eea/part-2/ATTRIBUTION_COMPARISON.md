# Attribution is not a single fact: comparative case evidence

This reader-facing comparison uses the existing Part 1 case and relationship registers. Its purpose is to explain what different types of source evidence establish about organisations. It is **not** a ranking of ideologies or an exhaustive catalogue of incidents.

| Case | Ideological or organisational proposition | Evidence stage | What remains unproven in the reviewed material |
|---|---|---|---|
| Germany, 2021 ICE attack | Court found a radical jihadist motive and a solo decision | Court finding as summarised in the annual relationship register | Membership, assistance or direction by an organisation |
| Denmark, 2021 Holbæk plot | PET reports a terrorism-attempt and financing conviction, plus a separate finding involving communication-account assistance to an IS fighter | Official agency report of appellate judgment | That an external organisation commanded the plot |
| Sweden, November 2021 case | Europol reports links to Nordic Resistance Movement (NMR) | Official report of association | Membership, material assistance, direction or an NMR organisational decision |
| Germany, Solingen, 2024 | German court findings include IS membership; Europol separately reports a claim and messaging | Final court outcome reported by BGH; separate Europol claim reporting | Operational command or financing of the attack |
| Greece, Hellenic Train, 2025 | Europol reports Revolutionary Class Self-Defense claim; NCTC attributes the act to the group | Official report of claim plus intelligence attribution | Named participants and Greek judicial determination of responsibility |
| Italy, Rome, 2022 | Europol reports a claim under a FAI/FRI-associated label | Official report of responsibility claim | Identity of actors, stable membership or central direction |
| Italy, Rome Tesla fire, 2025 | RAI reports an anarchist responsibility claim and investigation | Media report of claim and investigation | Verified perpetrator identity or organisational affiliation |
| Italy, Milan Unareti, 2025 | Italian prosecutor-general account reports a claim in an insurrectionary anarchist context | Official report of claim | Claimant identity and operational command |
| Italy, Castel Gandolfo and Albano, 2025 | Police announce a custody order covering two incidents and allegations against the same suspect | Official pretrial decision | A conviction, a final terrorism classification or a wider organisational link |
| Austria, Vienna Westbahnhof, 2025 | DSN reports online contact with a purported ISKP member | Official security assessment; appellate sentencing outcome separately verified | Whether the contact was genuinely ISKP, or constituted financing or central command |

## Five distinct relationship questions

1. **Ideological affinity:** Does a source document ideas or expressions? This does not establish a group connection.
2. **Claimed responsibility:** Did an actor or label claim an act? This documents the claim, not necessarily its truth.
3. **Association or communication:** Is contact reported or established? This does not automatically prove membership or command.
4. **Membership or assistance:** Has a competent court or another specifically identified source established a defined organisational relationship? Its scope and procedural status must be recorded.
5. **Operational direction:** Is there evidence that someone instructed, controlled or financed the particular act? Do not infer this from any of the first four propositions.

The Solingen and 2021 ICE records are a useful contrast: both involve a jihadist context, but the reviewed court records support materially different organisational relationships. The same distinction applies to FAI/FRI, NMR and Greek anarchist claims. Ideological similarity cannot fill an evidentiary gap.
