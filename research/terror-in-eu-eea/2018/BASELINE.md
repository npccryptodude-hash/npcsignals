Europol's **TE-SAT 2019**, published 27 June 2019, covers calendar 2018. The full publisher PDF was retrieved 18 November 2026. It has 82 pages, 11,874,700 bytes, DOI **10.2813/788404** and SHA-256 `e233dd615a6d97ace37802e2372f4fbc5a43cf68368b11530b117e2158bcf0d2`. The publication page records a 6 December 2021 update; the checksum describes the retrieved file. The [source register](sources.csv) includes the original download and the European Parliament copy.

The category and country totals reconcile to p. 68. Jihadist outcomes use pp. 14, 30–32 and the annex note. Macerata is the completed right-wing attack (pp. 60, 68). The remaining categories have no verified complete outcome distribution at country/category level. Attack completion in individual narratives does not resolve all slots in the same cell.

**Dutch discrepancy.** Annex 2 shows four jihadist events and prints another four in the Dutch unspecified cell, with a row total of four. The unspecified column total is one, assigned to Italy, and other annex margins, the overview, and the Dutch narrative support four Dutch jihadist events (two completed, two foiled). Counting the additional printed four would give 133. The country-cell transcription uses four jihadist and zero unspecified; the printed anomaly survives in [statistics.csv](statistics.csv).

Nine contributors report attacks: UK 60, France 30, Italy 13, Spain 11, Greece seven, Netherlands four, Germany two, Belgium and Sweden one each. Annex 3 p. 69 records 1,056 arrests in 18 countries: 511 jihadist, 44 right-wing, 34 left-wing, 30 separatist, three single-issue and 434 unspecified. Belgium reports 166, of which 17 are coded jihadist and 149 are unspecified. The annex says most of the latter relate to jihadist activity. They remain unspecified in the register. UK arrests also remain unspecified.

Europol reports **13 victim deaths and 53 injuries**, of which 46 injuries relate to jihadist attacks (pp. 14, 30, 68). All headline deaths are attributed to jihadist attacks. The named death counts reconcile to Carcassonne/Trèbes four, Paris one, Liège three and Strasbourg five. The original headline can therefore be derived as 13 deaths at fixed EU27 geography. A compatible total UK injury subtraction was not verified. Methods on pp. 78–80 cover national inputs, national classifications where the directive leaves room, and judicial arrests authorised by a prosecutor or investigating judge. Eurojust proceedings and thematic examples are separate observations.

The [31-country coverage](coverage.csv) preserves missing, no-row, outside-scope and incompatible data. [Incident allocations](crosswalk.csv) have 22 adequately identified slots and 107 unidentified. The Hague counts in the original publication and has a separate later classification record.
