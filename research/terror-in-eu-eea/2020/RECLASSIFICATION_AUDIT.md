# German judicial reclassification — 18 November 2026

**Established official revision:** TE-SAT 2021 p. 99 originally reports 57 EU attacks and four German jihadist attacks. TE-SAT 2022 retains 57 (p. 7). TE-SAT 2023 p. 9 n. 4 is the earliest revision located and gives 56, attributing it to a German court ruling removing one earlier jihadist classification. Its historical outcome comparison yields nine rather than ten completed jihadist events, with four foiled in both versions. That supports a completed removal by inference. No case identifier appears in the Europol footnote.

**Strong candidate: Berlin A100, 18 August 2020.** The same physical event is named among the completed cases in TE-SAT 2021 p. 44, citing the joint police/prosecution announcement of 19 August 2020 (n. 52). The Landgericht Berlin ruling of 31 January 2022, **521 Ks 1/21**, fits the timing. The court's announcement of 1 February describes conduct amounting to attempted murder, acute psychosis excluding criminal responsibility, and hospital placement under § 63 StGB. It expressly rejects characterisation as an Islamist attack. The judicial account acknowledges religious and Islamist elements mixed into delusion. It gives no basis for concluding that the event did not occur or that Europol's original record was duplicated. The ruling lacked finality at announcement. BGH dismissed the revision on 16 February 2023 (4 StR 329/22) and announced finality on 22 February 2023. See the [Phase 19 court source and disposition](../audit/PHASE_19_REMEDIATION.md#tests-and-primary-source-findings).

The federal constitutional protection report and the subsequent CTC review corroborate the change between the initial national assessment and the court account. Dresden's OLG judgment for murder and attempted murder and the Waldkraiburg federal prosecution case supply alternative comparisons for the original cohort. The Hamburg constitutional protection report also records that the Munich OLG treated both jihadist ideology and mental illness as contributing factors in Waldkraiburg. Neither comparison supplied a similar express rejection in the documents located. The German foiled cell remains separately identified; the unchanged foiled margin makes removal from that cell less consistent with the later aggregate. None of the comparisons substitutes for the national correction or an Europol case-level cross-reference.
