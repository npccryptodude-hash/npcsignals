## Europol and Eurojust

The historical TE-SAT 2021 methodology p. 107 documents collection, agency processing, cross-checking with Member States, correction or supplementation of gaps and divergences, and Member State validation. The historical annex describes judicial arrests warranted by prosecutors or investigating judges and retains them after provisional release or house arrest. TE-SAT 2026 introduction and nn. 1–5 explain that incident statistics reflect Member States' terrorism classifications under national laws. Separately reported violent-extremism examples may provide narrative context and are excluded from the quantitative terrorism overview. Europol's ideology headings are analytical categories. Directive 2017/541 art. 3 supplies the substantive EU legal framework without defining an annual row-by-row TE-SAT coding manual. The report itself gives the completed, failed and foiled outcomes (pp. 18–20, 47). The sources examined supply no common public decision rule for borderline instances across all contributors. A domestic attempt offence can coexist with a description of an attack as carried out in an administrative report.

Article 7 of Regulation 2016/794 establishes national units and liaison and allows designated direct contacts subject to national conditions. Council Decision 2005/671/JHA art. 2 identifies specialised authorities and a framework for transmitting terrorism investigation information. These are legal exchange mechanisms. The public documents reviewed do not identify which authority filed each annual row, its transmission channel, the format, any preprocessing or case key, a submission freeze, or an automatic feed from police data. E-002096/2023 confirms that Europol owns the assessment and that the Commission cannot double-check the underlying facts and data. Do not project the 2021 operational description in full onto 2026.

Corrections can be observed in later outputs. TE-SAT 2023 p. 9 n. 4 records a German removal after a court ruling and three subsequently reported French cases for 2021. TE-SAT 2024 full edition pp. 11–12 nn. 12/15 and p. 51 n. 75 records the French 2024 transmission of 2021–2022 events into its 2023 reference cohort. These are documented timing differences. The per-record amendment path, notification date and rules determining the edition are unknown. Record occurrence, national classification, submission, report reference and release dates in separate fields where available.

Eurojust has a distinct judicial channel. The TE-SAT 2021 appendix documents contributions of templates or legal materials and national-desk verification of its 2020 judicial contribution. Regulation 2023/2131, art. 1 inserting art. 21a in Regulation 2018/1727, provides a later judicial-information framework. TE-SAT 2026 pp. 23–24 nn. 31–33 defines the published subset, including unresolved appeals from some states and the latest judgment when an appeal concludes within the year. It counts one verdict per person within a proceeding even if several terrorism offences are involved. The 406 convictions and 99 acquittals are contributed outcomes, with repeat people across proceedings or years untested. A same-year quotient against 486 arrests would mix different populations and timing.

## Germany

The BKA 2024 PMK factsheet, p. 3, documents transmission by Landeskriminalämter to BKA, motive/context classification at intake, multiple descriptive fields and supplemental reports on significant developments including known prosecutorial or court decisions. The published 2024 snapshot is dated 31 January 2025. Berlin Senate 19/21271, preliminary note pp. 1–2, describes an offence-date case unit that can encompass several acts, suspects, provisions or investigations in a closely linked set of facts. It expressly allows changes within or after the year. PMK is broader than terrorism, and the report distinguishes terrorism, violence, propaganda and other offence types. The German federal replies on Islamist attacks describe a further ongoing, individual assessment for completed, prevented and technically failed events. The May 2026 reply covers prevented cases only and distinguishes pending reviews from already classified events. The annual NRW intelligence report states a nationwide five under its jihadist-motivated wording. It names Essen and Bielefeld, and supplies no complete national roster or reconciliation on the cited pages.

A100 demonstrates a named 2020 Europol example, the lower-court ruling, confirmed finality and an unnamed later EU deletion. The general PMK amendment procedure makes a domestic change possible. Neither an A100 amendment in PMK nor automatic propagation into TE-SAT has been established. For 2025, four federal named locations, five in the NRW nationwide statement and four in the EU completed-jihadist cell remain three observations. Published differences in unit, stage and scope make simple cardinality matching unsafe, but do not identify a missing case or explain the numeric gap. Essen's age at offence remains a distinct judicial observation. A publication date or age at trial cannot substitute for the undisclosed Europol age reference.

## France

Justice Ministry circular JUSD2003946C, pp. 2–5, documents early local-prosecutor contact with PNAT on possible terrorist qualification, fact-specific review of intent, terrorist enterprise and purpose, and later reassessment in light of judicial investigation. The circular explains specialised centralisation in practice. Use the Code de procédure pénale art. 706-17 for concurrent jurisdiction and Code pénal art. 421-1 for the substantive link to an enterprise aimed at seriously disturbing public order by intimidation or terror. The text covers an intake and prosecutorial decision path, including where police take investigative steps. It supplies no rule equating one prosecution referral with one physical attack or one TE-SAT row.

PNAT's 2025 activity document has a referral series and a separate account of three committed and seven foiled jihadist-inspired attacks. The 16 October DNRT hearing gives a dated intelligence assessment and six preventive arrests attributed to the relevant services. Those are distinct from seven thwarted attacks and from Europol's two completed and eight foiled French entries. The sources identify national roles and some named incidents. They leave the annual EU submitter, export and deduplication rules and the coding of the three competing candidates undocumented. French referral counts of four and 22 happen to equal the older separatist contributions. The 2023 referral count of 74 and the 44 occurrence-year records show that this equality cannot establish a shared population. The October 2023 ministry response counts 32 destruction or attempted-destruction facts across police and gendarmerie jurisdictions, with one named Ajaccio fact. The 34 described by Europol cannot be reconciled by the reference window alone. This overnight comparison is distinct from the 26 retrospective records.

## Discrepancy and denominator decisions

**New 2023 arrest appendix observation.** The original TE-SAT 2024 full edition p. 63 reports 426 and category totals 334 + 26 + 14 + 25 + 18 + 9. TE-SAT 2026 p. 18 retains 426. Its retrospective annex pp. 49–52 independently gives 334 + 31 + 14 + 25 + 27 = **431**. The difference lies in the right-wing column. The original lists Belgium four, Croatia two and Lithuania one. The later table shows Belgium twelve and has no Croatia or Lithuania row, yielding the net change of five with the other visible 2023 right-wing entries matching. Missing rows describe an omission from that table, with no implication of zero nationally. No keyed correction or explanation for the discrepancy was found. Preserve 426 in the baseline and disclose 431 as a sum of later printed category tables. Do not classify it as an official revision. Cross-year right-wing arrest graphics need version labels and this caveat.

**2025 age.** TE-SAT 2026 p. 23 n. 30 separately repeats that 15 jihadist arrests had unspecified age, consistent with p. 27 n. 36 on that missing count. It does not establish the 344 denominator used in n. 36 or resolve the conflict with 347 on pp. 21–22, 27 and 49. The six missing offence classifications on p. 21 n. 23 are a different missing field. No age share is calculated.

**Other denominator controls.** TE-SAT 2019 p. 69 records Belgium's 166 as 17 in the jihadist table cell and 149 unspecified, alongside a note on the contribution. Keep both coded groups and the original total. The UK's 273 excludes 148 Northern Ireland arrests according to that same annex. An arrest is a reported suspect measure, with no basis here for treating it as a unique person across years, a perpetrator count or the denominator for same-year judicial outcomes. Attack rows can group related acts or count separate sites. Completed outcomes can include no-injury cases. Fatalities and injury counts need victim scope, case grouping, treatment threshold and cutoff. The 2016 contradiction (379 overall against 597 across three case narratives) remains unresolved. In 2025 six fatalities in all completed cases and five fatalities plus 81 injuries for the jihadist category have different scopes, and severe injuries in a case narrative cannot be pooled as all injuries.

## Evidence priorities and publication controls

The [decision register](phase21_evidence_priorities.csv) assigns one primary disposition per gap and states the trigger to resume. Public, named documents can support case-specific judicial stages and published aggregate comparisons. Exact A100 removal, national-to-EU 2025 links and the French retrospective or overnight event joins are held pending keyed primary evidence or an official case-naming crosswalk. The 2015 graphic, 2016 injury conflict, 2023 retrospective arrests and 2025 age denominator need new publisher evidence to move beyond a documented discrepancy. Aggregate totals cannot determine a fifth German incident, the pairing of the French slots, or scope-equivalent counts of unique people.

| Evidence layer | Control for use |
|---|---|
| Reported terrorism statistics | Visualise published values by reference year and edition, annotate geography, late entries and discrepancies. Fixed EU27 supports a geographic comparison of reported cohorts. |
| National administrative classification | Show the issuing institution, date, definition and cases or counts in its own scope. |
| Judicially established facts | Use the precise offence, stage, holding and verified finality for the relevant person or case. |
| Intelligence assessment | Attribute claims to the agency and the date and disclose assessment scope. |
| Physical incident reconstruction | Show verified case extracts and unidentified coverage. Missing identities leave occurrence evidence incomplete. |
| Analytical estimates | Label the occurrence-year sensitivity and its transfer assumptions. Display it separately from official revisions. |

A four-layer historical graphic is usable with clear provenance for the original counts, fixed geography, later official revisions and conditional sensitivity. Injury trends, unique-person measures, conviction conversion rates and comprehensive physical attack incidence remain unsupported. No changes to publication authorisation.
