## Reading and reproducing the layers

The [machine-readable reconciliation](data/eu_statistical_reconciliation_2015_2025.csv) records both original totals, the included UK rows, matching confidence counts and annual report locators. For 2015–2019 subtract the included UK values per measure. For 2020 onward the annual edition already separates UK, so the fixed subset uses a zero subtraction. The zeros in the subtraction columns describe contributions to the EU aggregate, not UK activity. The fixed sums preserve each edition's reporting dates and classifications. See the [2015 geography audit](2015/GEOGRAPHY_AUDIT.md) and the chain of annual [2016–2025 comparisons](2016/COMPARABILITY_2016_2025.md).

The [provenance ledger](2021/provenance.csv) records the official later 2020 and 2021 revisions and the French historical cohorts. TE-SAT 2023 p. 9 n. 4 explains the 2020 removal (57 to 56) and three additional 2021 French foiled right-wing records (15 to 18). Berlin A100 remains a strong candidate for the removal. TE-SAT 2024 includes 26 older French attacks in its 2023 reporting cohort and already specifies four occurred in 2021 and 22 in 2022. TE-SAT 2026 corroborates this split and explicitly gives 44 occurrences in 2023. Thus the sensitivity row adds three and four to 2021, adds 22 to 2022, removes 26 from 2023, and removes one from 2020. Cohort IDs are absent, so the sensitivity assumes distinct late entries and cannot test all overlap. The [2015 alternate chart](2015/provenance_evidence.csv) reports 193 without the evidence needed for a formal revision. The later official revision field for 2015 remains empty.

The original 2019–2020 arrow of 119 to 57 becomes 55 to 57 on fixed EU27 geography. Even geography-normalised counts retain variations in national law, reporting practice, thresholds for foiled plots, category coding and retrospective additions. UK and Northern Ireland national series have their own metric and period. The 31-country register does not provide a harmonised attack census. Arrests refer to suspects and may include offences unrelated to recorded attacks or to that year's attack cohort.

Victim figures are preserved in annual source registers. Some older totals are open bounds; some published case injury counts and aggregate figures are internally incompatible, notably 2016. Headline figures that exclude Northern Ireland casualties may still pair with attack tables including its security incidents. Unknown country/category outcome allocations remain unknown. Neither a 31-country injury series nor a pooled completion rate is calculated.
