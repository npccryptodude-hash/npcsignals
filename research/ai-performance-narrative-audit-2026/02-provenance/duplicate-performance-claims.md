# Duplicate performance claims

## Purpose

This file records the cross-case provenance pattern without converting repetition into actor attribution.

## Observed pattern

During the audit, multiple AI-performance narratives were identified with unusually specific first-person phrasing and overlapping numerical or descriptive detail.

The strongest documented example is MEMEMOON, where materially similar first-person descriptions appeared across different accounts.

Other duplicate-claim candidates were identified during discovery, but several rely primarily on archived or secondary material and are therefore not promoted to the core v1.0 finding set without stronger source capture.

## Analytical rule

Repeated text can support a provenance finding when the overlap is specific enough to exceed generic topical similarity.

It does not by itself establish:

- common control
- shared authorship
- coordinated distribution
- fabricated underlying performance
- financial relationship between the accounts

## Evidence treatment

A repeated narrative is not counted as multiple independent confirmations of the underlying trade.

Instead, duplication can reduce evidentiary independence.

This is central to the audit's distinction:

**distribution depth is not evidence depth.**

## Current conclusion

The audit supports a recurring content-provenance problem in at least one strongly documented case.

The broader prevalence of duplicated AI-performance narratives across the full ecosystem remains unmeasured.
