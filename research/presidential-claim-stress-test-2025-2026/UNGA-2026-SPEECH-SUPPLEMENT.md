## Speech-level observation

The speech contains a mixture of:

- claims strongly supported by current official data,
- claims with a correct empirical core but stronger historical framing than the evidence cleanly supports,
- claims whose wording lacks the denominator or definition needed for reproducible scoring,
- and political/evaluative statements that should not be forced into a factual-accuracy ledger.

Two examples illustrate the distinction.

### Crime

The FBI reported that violent crime fell sharply from 2024 to 2025 and described the decline as the largest year-to-year decrease since its national estimates began in 1936.

That supports the core of UNGA26-05.

The separate claim that the murder rate was the lowest in 125 years is less clean. The FBI's 2025 rate of 4.1 per 100,000 ties 1955 and 1956 in its historical series, while comparisons extending back to 1900 combine different data sources and less-comparable methodologies.

### U.N. budget

A 15.1% reduction appeared in U.N. revised budget estimates for 2026, alongside large proposed staffing reductions. The final budget outcome is not identical to that proposal: later reporting described a 9.2% reduction in the 2026 Secretariat budget.

The speech therefore compresses a real reform and proposed reduction into a stronger final-outcome formulation.
